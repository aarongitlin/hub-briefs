## Items (max 3)

### Google puts an AI product-Q&A agent inside YouTube ads — but keeps checkout separate
- What happened: Google opened a US beta (announced Sept 16) embedding its Gemini-powered Business Agent directly into YouTube video ads for eligible Demand Gen campaigns tied to Merchant Center feeds (min $10/day, English mobile in-stream, Shorts excluded); viewers can ask product questions without leaving the video. Trade coverage is explicit that purchasing inside the ad itself is not part of this beta — checkout still routes through Google's separate UCP flow, which gained a new "cart transfer to merchant site" option the same week.
- So what for Shopping Ads: The team that owns your platform's shopping-ad roadmap just shipped ambient product Q&A inside video without ambient checkout — treat that gap as their considered position, not a rollout lag, and plan the in-video experience around research/intent-capture rather than assuming one-tap purchase is next.
- Thesis signal: UNDERMINES — Google decoupled conversational research from the purchase step in its own flagship ambient-video product, the clearest first-party signal yet that in-video checkout isn't imminent even from the platform best positioned to ship it.
- Horizon: RESPOND-NOW
- Confidence: Google's own blog (blog.google, Sept 16) confirms the beta and eligibility; the explicit "no in-ad purchasing" detail comes from trade press (Search Engine Land, ContentGrip) rather than a direct Google quote — treat that specific claim as secondary-sourced.
- Prior coverage: DELTA ON BRIEF 2026-07-27 — sharpens "Google's Universal Cart still hasn't reached YouTube": the research layer (Business Agent) is now landing on YouTube ads first, while the cart layer still hasn't, widening rather than closing that gap.

### 132 million Americans now shop with AI help — 59% of those purchases still close on Amazon
- What happened: PYMNTS Intelligence's new survey (2,061 consumers, 60 merchants, fielded Sept 2025–Aug 2026, published Sept 15) finds 132 million US adults have used AI to assist a retail purchase and 46% use it to hunt deals, but 59% of AI-assisted purchases still conclude on Amazon and only 3% of actual transactions involve an agent executing the buy.
- So what for Shopping Ads: Wherever the "moment of intent" now happens — search, chat, or eventually video — the sale still tends to land on one destination platform; any video-ad-to-purchase flow should assume it's competing for the discovery step, not the close, until that ratio moves.
- Thesis signal: UNDERMINES — AI is expanding as a discovery layer but consolidating rather than diffusing where the transaction actually completes.
- Horizon: FUTURECAST
- Confidence: PYMNTS is a trade-research firm that sells sponsored studies; this report's methodology isn't fully disclosed in the public write-up, and the "who commissioned it" question is unresolved.
- Prior coverage: NEW — first hard consolidated number behind the pattern Etsy ("<1% of traffic") and Target ("still small in total") already gestured at in their Q2 earnings calls.

### Pinterest's brand-new ad product still redirects to checkout, not in-feed purchase
- What happened: At Pinterest Presents (Sept 17), Pinterest launched Visual Search Ads — beta rolling out across all ad markets over the coming weeks — placing ads inside visual search results and pin closeups, citing 80B+ monthly searches ("the vast majority... visual, and more than half... commercial"). Per Pinterest's own newsroom post, the ads route users to "an advertiser's site" to complete the purchase.
- So what for Shopping Ads: A visual-content platform built a shopping-ad product from a blank slate in 2026 and still chose discovery-then-redirect over in-feed checkout — treat that as a live architecture choice by a peer, not legacy debt, when arguing for or against in-video purchase completion internally.
- Thesis signal: UNDERMINES — even a greenfield ad product with no legacy checkout stack defaults to the destination model.
- Horizon: RESPOND-NOW
- Confidence: Single-sourced to Pinterest's own newsroom; the 80B-search figure is unverified against independent data.
- Prior coverage: NEW
