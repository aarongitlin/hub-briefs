
## [[2026-10-25]]

### Commerce related

- Another "shop the look" feature I hadn't encountered before from [NBCUniversal](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/nbcuniversals-shoppable-tv-business-is-seeing-a-boost-from-shows-like-traitors-and-love-island-usa/) From an interview: 
	- "When we associate shoppable moments to the IP that fans care about, they engage [with it] at a significantly higher level, both in terms of initial engagement and actual shopping behavior. And also, the brand sentiment, favorability [and] recall — all those things go up when we can refer to or relate to a brand.
		- Sounds to me like this is very seriously curated, and much more of a brand play than a performance play
	- The have a [marketplace](https://shopthemoment.tv/) that brands can contribute to, and NBCU may or may not use them. They have an API that partners like LG can use to create viewer experiences. I can't find many examples of how this actually looks though.

Second, when we bring that shopping experience [out] from within the ad experience — actually [putting it] in the show — we see an even greater response from the consumer. … And then, finally, the easier we can make it for the consumer to go from what they’re seeing on screen to being able to shop the products they want, the higher the engagement. That actually got us investing more in remote interactivity on screen.”"
- I like the concept of the "feedvisor" from [[weekly-2026-10-25]] - people get the recommendations to consider from AI, but need to go elsewhere to validate, especially price. Speaks to agents as a helpful mid-funnel tool, but lacking as both early funnel or late funnel [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/ai-strategies/ai-boosts-discovery-but-reviews-still-seal-the-deal-new-research-shows/)
	- I wonder if UCP / quick buy could actually help with the transaction and price part. But how do you know its actually a good deal? Feels like without *something* the ability to purchase has limited value
- Walmart is building "Agent Gateway" - which sounds like walmart for agents. I wonder why they are leaning in so much, while amazon is blocking so hard. Maybe walmart can land on physical stores as its experience, and online as purely transactional. It's pretty onerous though, each agent requires specific authorization from walmart, and the user to adjust settings. Will people really do that? Is this just a way to say they are embracing agents without really embracing them?
	- From the [techcrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/21/while-amazon-blocks-ai-agents-walmart-rolls-out-the-welcome-mat/) article: "customers will be able to authorize each AI agent they use with a dedicated profile in their account, allowing them to see the agent’s purchases separately from their own. Plus, customers will be in control of how purchases get approved by either requiring approval for each transaction or by authorizing transactions within a certain spending limit they set"

### UX Related

- The [mattrothenberg.com](https://mattrothenberg.com/notes/pure-ui-agentic-era/) article and Claude's synthesis: "Make display-relevant state an explicit page input, so there's a list to check against" reminded me of a recommendation for a book: [The Checklist Manifesto](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=checklist+manifesto&hvadid=792678962575&hvdev=c&hvexpln=67&hvlocphy=9030944&hvnetw=g&hvocijid=15510328373595436074--&hvqmt=e&hvrand=15510328373595436074&hvtargid=kwd-301159702258&hydadcr=23952_13896466&mcid=732d1db5bb5d36a2bb1c1620534c84b3&tag=googhydr-20&ref=pd_sl_8xlr4y7lpx_e_p67) I haven't read it but the creator who talked about it mentioned it in the context of systems thinking, and gave examples of airlines and hospitals utilizing it. It's painfully obvious, but there are tricks: like don't make a checklist for something that is never skipped.
