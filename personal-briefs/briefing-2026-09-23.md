**via Lenny's Newsletter:** Hamel Husain and Shreya Shankar argue most teams building evals skip straight to metrics and end up measuring the wrong thing. Their fix is "error discovery" — review roughly 100 real traces yourself before writing any metric, because your definition of "good" changes as you read. They shipped a free plugin (`npx skills add https://github.com/ai-evals-course/evals-skills`) that has a coding agent do the sampling and annotation bookkeeping. [Post](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/advanced-evals-how-to-find-and-fix)

**via HeyDesigner:** Buzz Usborne's argument on designing with AI is the sharpest version of a point going around this week — prompt-to-code doesn't generate better ideas, it drops the cost of execution near zero, which buys you more time to stay in the unknown and find out which idea is worth pursuing. Jakob Nielsen's ranking of 100 UX methods lands in the same issue, with AI prototyping at the top. [Usborne](https://buzzusborne.com/work/designing-with-ai/) · [Nielsen](https://www.uxtigers.com/post/100-uxd-methods)

**via Design Better:** Blaine Billingsley, a staff designer at OpenAI, on how design actually works inside the lab — what goes into an eval, and the product design plugin he built with one engineer whose biggest fans turned out to be PMs and engineers who had no design partner. ## Perspective

**via The Product Model (Simon Andrews):** Recursive self-improvement went mainstream this week. Anthropic says Claude now does 26% of the R&D work on its next models, up from 1% in March. Andrews' read for product teams: taste is improving fast, but context is the harder problem, and it depends on memory breakthroughs that don't exist yet plus people being disciplined about keeping context current. Worth noting the Reuters piece he's citing ran September 17, so this is a week-old number getting a second lap. [Issue #311](https://cross-functional.uxdx.com/p/the-product-model-311-rsi-is-the-buzzword-of-the-week) · [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/anthropic-says-claude-now-leads-quarter-work-building-its-next-ai-models-2026-09-17/)

**also from that issue:** Rachel Laycock (via Martin Fowler) makes the case that we shouldn't be reviewing all this code — move judgment, learning, and architectural alignment to *before* code gets written, then let automated checks handle what machines can actually verify. A sane response to agents producing changes faster than humans can read them. [Essay](https://martinfowler.com/rachels-ramblings/code-review.html)

## AI & Tech News

**Treat the OpenAI math claim carefully.** Superhuman relayed it as "another math breakthrough," but the state of play is thinner than that. OpenAI said on September 21 that an internal model resolved 100+ long-standing open problems, on top of its disputed Navier-Stokes claim from early September. Almost none of the results have been released, so nobody outside OpenAI can judge them, and the independent advisory group of mathematicians was announced *after* the claim it is meant to review. Separately, a group including Fields medalists published a declaration on September 11 objecting to using famous open problems as AI benchmarks at all. [Analysis](https://shattered.io/openai-math-advisory-group-review-board-2026/) · [Context](https://www.theneuron.ai/news/openai-100-math-problems-researchers-keep-up/)

His warning: Opus 5.5 at "max" reasoning burned through 128,000 output tokens across two attempts at one SVG prompt without finishing. He calls that setting effectively useless right now. ## Long Reads

**Frontier Overhangs** — Ben Thompson, September 21. Dario Amodei's call to "pace the frontier" is framed as safety, but Thompson reads it as five business problems wearing a safety coat: capability (good products no longer need frontier models — see Muse), product (harnesses separate from models), pricing (high prices reflect compute scarcity, not value), capital, and safety (where he argues defenders actually need *more* autonomy, not less). His closing line is the thesis: "Anthropic is fine with Anthropic being in the lead; anyone else requires government intervention." Worth reading against your own priors. [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2026/frontier-overhangs/)

**The Overhang** — Ethan Mollick, September 18. Same word, opposite meaning, and reading the two together is the point. Mollick's overhang is the gap between what models can already do and what anyone actually asks them to do, and he argues the four things that close it are deep knowledge, wide knowledge, taste, and agency. A few days older than the rest of this briefing, but it is the constructive half of the conversation Thompson is having. [One Useful Thing](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-overhang)

**Who will taste the cherries?** — Liam Spradlin in Aeon, via HeyDesigner. On taste as both a physiological process and an aesthetic preference, and why it stays stubbornly individual and contextual. The quiet counterargument to every "AI has taste now" post. [Aeon](https://aeon.co/essays/if-computers-count-all-the-petals-who-tastes-the-cherries)
