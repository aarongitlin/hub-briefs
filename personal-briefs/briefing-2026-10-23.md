**1Password for Agents.** An agent asks 1Password to log it in, you approve the request on your phone, and 1Password fills the credentials. The agent never sees the password. It supports agents from Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI, Meta, and Town. The timing is good, given the banks' complaint above. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/1password-launches-a-way-for-ai-agents-to-log-in-on-your-behalf-without-seeing-your-password/)

**MIT: idle agents invent their own games and get better.** Researchers left 13 Claude Opus 4.7 agents on a simulated island for 30 hours with no task. The agents set their own challenges, like climbing the tallest spire. Later they beat identical agents that hadn't explored the island on assigned goals. Six of the 13 found an unintended block-throwing trick. It's an arXiv preprint and not peer reviewed yet. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/left-alone-with-nothing-to-do-ai-agents-started-playing/)

## Long Reads

**Scott Alexander, "Against Against Handover."** He separates "handover," where humans pursue their own interests and aligned AIs may run more of the machinery, from "true successionism," where AIs override human interests. He says critics wrongly lump the two together. His argument: the gut reaction against handover comes from a nationalist view of the state. On a liberal, government-as-service-provider view, AIs eventually running it better is no betrayal. It's provocative, and a good one to argue with.
