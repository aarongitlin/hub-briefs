# Daily Briefing — October 16, 2026

## From Your Inbox

**via Superhuman AI:** Meta released the weights for Muse Spark Open, one of the strongest American-made open models, optimized for Apple silicon. The catch: you need at least two top-spec M5 Ultra Mac Studios to run it. Meta's Alexandr Wang told Axios "we're excited for mommy and daddy to be playing nice again," which is as close to a public thaw with Apple as you'll get. [Meta research blog](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-spark-open)

**via Superhuman AI:** Nick Cammarata, ex-OpenAI interpretability researcher now at Goodfire, published an informal essay arguing the field was wrong that models are nearly impossible to inspect directly. He blames flawed architectures and weak tooling, not the networks themselves. If it holds up, pre-release understanding of model behavior gets cheaper. [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/)

**via Superhuman AI:** Roughly 400 scientists across 34 countries signed an open letter calling for binding AI oversight, arguing the voluntary safety accord US labs signed weeks ago is not enough. Their proposal: isolate frontier models from external networks and put them under independent review. Hinton and Bengio are both on it. [Coverage](https://qz.com/scientists-international-body-advanced-ai-oversight-101326)

**via Superhuman AI:** Ramp opened Glass, the internal AI workspace it built after spending $1.5M on frontier models in a single week. It is multiplayer and accumulates institutional memory. 323 employees made 6,500 edits to what their own instance knows. [Early access](https://ramp.com/glass)

**via Fix / Insider:** Anthropic told the FT that models could act as self-replicating "worms" spreading across consumer devices like TVs and lawnmowers, starting slow, "akin to slime mold or a swarm of fruit flies." Their framing: no full rogue replicator yet, but agents can already do the individual steps, so harden systems now. [FT](https://www.ft.com/content/64aaa8d5-f26a-4b96-b8cf-0ef0c6fbe69e)

**via Fix / Insider:** Netflix is laying off 1,000 staff, with results due in a couple of weeks and the share price already soft. Simon frames it against Amazon's Mike Hopkins talking up Prime Video's theatrical slate and appetite for more sports rights. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/14/netflix-to-lay-off-1000-staff-amid-intensifying-competition-for-user-attention/)

*One caveat on Fix:* it relays Instinct's $1B Series C at a $10B valuation as current news. The TechCrunch piece it links is dated September 28, about three weeks old.

**via Lenny's Newsletter:** A cross-post from Nikhyl Singhal's The Skip argues job-search advice now expires every six months. Nikhyl re-records this episode roughly every hundred days because the market keeps moving under it. [Post](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/cp/220579699)

## Perspective

**via John Cutler (TBM 445):** Ten years after the original "Feature Factory" post, Cutler's retrospective mostly argues against his younger self. You are always a feature factory to some degree, the outcomes-vs-outputs binary was a useful rhetorical device that then wrecked a lot of orgs, and "performative outcome-centricity" ships unusable work under better vocabulary. ## AI & Tech News

**Anthropic's IPO math is getting a reality check.** The FT reports Anthropic told investors Q3 revenue hit $16.8bn, up nearly 50%, and that distillation by rival labs accounted for 5-7% of revenue before being curbed. Reuters separately reports investors tempering expectations on the valuation premium. Earlier reporting had floated a $2 trillion target. No timetable or price has been announced. [Forge tracker](https://forgeglobal.com/insights/anthropic-upcoming-ipo-news/) · [CEO Today](https://www.ceotodaymagazine.com/2026/10/anthropic-ipo-valuation-expectations-cool-as-investors-weigh-growth/)

**Anthropic is giving METR permanent employee-level access.** Outside evaluators get to look under the hood rather than poke at an API. This is the concrete version of a commitment labs have been making in principle for two years, and a direct answer to the critique that external evals are theater. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/category/artificial-intelligence/)

**Replit's CEO is pitching the "self-driving company."** Amjad Masad's claim is that the org chart, not the codebase, is what agents eventually automate. Worth reading as a position rather than a forecast. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/category/artificial-intelligence/)

**Google's Universal Cart shipped in the US.** A cross-retailer cart is the unglamorous infrastructure that agentic shopping actually needs. Note the timing against Airbnb's argument, via the WSJ, that agents can find you a cheap Paris hotel but not a specific Catskills cabin. Supply that only exists in one place is the moat. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/category/artificial-intelligence/)

## Long Reads

**AI Is Mass-Decompiling Games, and That's Both Awesome and Scary** (Popcar). Model-driven decompilation is unlocking real preservation and mashup work on games whose source is long gone. The same capability makes copying live paid software trivial. A good piece on a capability that cuts both ways and has no clean policy answer. [Read](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/14/ai-is-mass-decompiling-games/)

**Redesigning source control at Anthropic.** Anthropic built its own Git alternative, Scion, plus a new code review system, after daily merged changes passed 30,000. The interesting part is not the tool, it is what volume does to review: at that rate human review stops being a gate and becomes a sampling problem. [Simon Willison's summary](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/13/) · [Register coverage](https://www.theregister.com/a/5302943)

**A Conversation With Ari Emanuel About Entertainment and AI** (Stratechery, Oct 15). Ben Thompson interviews the Endeavor chief on what AI does to talent representation and content economics. Paired with the Netflix layoffs above, it is the clearest read on an industry repricing its own labor. [Read](https://stratechery.com/)
