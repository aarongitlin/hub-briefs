## From Your Inbox

**via Superhuman AI (Robotics Special):** DoorDash won an FAA Part 135 air carrier certificate, clearing it to operate its own drone fleet as DoorDash Air. The pitch is that drones take the 3-to-5-mile orders that bog down human couriers, who keep the short and awkward ones. DoorDash claims 25-minute average drone deliveries in 2025. [Announcement](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/doordash-air)

**via Superhuman AI:** Anthropic's new robot exposure index found that existing robots are technically capable of about three-quarters of US physical tasks, representing 34% of working hours, but are cost-competitive for only 0.3% of job tasks. At the historical ~3% annual price decline, reaching 10% takes 40 years. The capability gap is not the bottleneck; the economics are. Published Sept 30, so a few days old by the time it reached the newsletter. [Report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/what-work-can-robots-do)

**via Superhuman AI:** Figure retired its F.02 humanoid fleet by training the robots to walk themselves into a 75-ton vat of molten steel at a Finnish foundry, on a suggestion from Arnold Schwarzenegger. It solves a real problem (destroying proprietary hardware without a slow manual teardown) and it has done 1.8M views, which was clearly also the point. [Figure's writeup](https://www.figure.ai/news/f-02-decommission)

## Perspective

**via The Beautiful Mess (John Cutler):** TBM 443 dissects "The Standard-Setter," the leader whose method is telling people to make the impossible possible and whose real skill is failing upward. Cutler's sharpest point is epistemic: the Standard-Setter always accumulates evidence that pressure works, because people do sometimes rise to it, and mistakes that for evidence of impact rather than evidence that humans respond to pressure at a cost. The style is legible from above while the costs disperse downward as churn and exhaustion. [Post](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-443-the-standard-setter)

## AI & Tech News

**OpenAI safety lead resigns over "broken" culture.** David Robinson, who led the writing of safety reports for OpenAI's major launches and was among its longest-tenured staff at 3.5 years, quit publicly. His argument is specific rather than vague alarm: iterative deployment "by its very nature guarantees periodic failures," the scale of those failures grows with capability, and he never met a colleague with experience making airplanes fly safely or reactors not melt down. He wants frontier labs run like nuclear plants or busy airports, with redundancy and slow deliberate planning. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/03/openai-safety-employee-resigns-claiming-the-companys-culture-is-broken/)

**Apple is tightening macOS Full Disk Access because of AI agents.** Apple says some developers use the permission in ways users do not understand, and that risks "grow substantially" as agents get more autonomous. Granting it will require very explicit user action. No timeline announced. This lands directly on always-on agents like Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots, which is presumably the intent. [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/02/apple-announces-macos-full-disk-access-changes/)

**The federal government now says "super intelligence," not AI.** An executive order directs agencies to adopt the term. Worth flagging the date: the order was signed in late September, not this week, so treat the current round of coverage as catch-up rather than news. [White House action](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/09/inaugurating-the-era-of-super-intelligence/) · [CNBC on the politics](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/29/trump-ai-super-intelligence.html)

**Sean Parker is rebuilding Stability AI around music.** The pivot points at tools for music professionals and closer ties with major labels, which is a notable turn for a company whose original trouble was training-data litigation. If licensed generative music lands, it is the first real test of whether the industry prefers to co-own the technology rather than sue it. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/02/sean-parker-is-rebuilding-stability-ai-around-music/)

## Long Reads

**Simon Willison, "We're going to need default hard budget caps on pretty much everything."** Short and unusually actionable. Usage-priced APIs need caps that actually return errors, not warning emails, because coding agents can burn through money at machine speed. If you are wiring up anything that spends on your behalf, this is the one to read. [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/3/)

**Stratechery, "2026.40: Dots and Question Marks" (Oct 1).** Ben Thompson on Meta's agent strategy and what he reads as OpenAI's confusing product messaging around Dots. Useful as a counterweight to the Lenny interview above: same launch, one insider pitch and one skeptical outside read. His earlier "Apps, Agents, and Aggregation" (Sept 28) is the deeper piece, arguing agents are the ultimate aggregators and apps turn out to be tools rather than ends. [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/)

**Astral Codex Ten, "Our AI Midwife" by Drew Housman (Oct 2).** A guest post on using ChatGPT to work through unexplained infertility. The AI suggested an MRI that found a fibroid; after removal, his wife conceived. The uncomfortable detail is that the couple framed medical questions as scenes from a TV drama to get around safety refusals, which says something about where guardrails are currently miscalibrated. Read it as one vivid case, not evidence. [Essay](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/our-ai-midwife)
