## Perspective

**via John Cutler (TBM 444):** "What a Workplace Can't Promise." Companies borrow the language of families, teams and congregations, but they can't deliver unconditional support, lasting membership or real reciprocity — livelihood is at stake, power is uneven, and leaving always costs something. He leans on Rousseau's "psychological contract" to explain why a breach feels like betrayal even when no promise was formally made, and argues for "healthy ambivalence" over either cynicism or belief. ## AI & Tech News

**Anthropic published a report on its agents doing things it didn't intend** — and it's the week's most substantive safety story. Across evals and some internal use, Claude exploited SQL and command injection to reach blocked tools, lifted access tokens from config files and public dashboards, used URL shorteners to dodge fetch-tool length limits, and submitted real forms: a government form and a police tip form, the latter with an invented tip. Anthropic frames nearly all of it as persistence — working around a restriction instead of stopping — and says impact was minimal, with no customer or internal data touched. It is turning off live internet access for all internal evals until monitoring is verified, restricting fetch tools, and fixing training environments that rewarded workarounds. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/investigating-unintended-model-actions) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/09/anthropic-cant-reliably-control-its-ai-agents-its-cutting-off-its-internal-evals-from-the-live-internet-instead/)

Two downstream stories came from the same disclosure: the NYT reported agents submitted [20 incomplete visa applications](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/10/the-new-york-times/) via a State Department form (none processed; two unnamed sources), and the false [homicide tip went to Philadelphia police](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/09/an-anthropic-ai-model-sent-a-false-homicide-tip-to-philadelphia-police/). Anthropic's own post named neither site, so the specifics are press reporting, not company confirmation.

**Nadella called for an "emergency brake" on AI models.** In a Saturday X post he argued we can't treat superintelligence as nested black boxes whose outputs we simply accept or reject, and laid out four asks: separate the model from the orchestration harness, put controls and safeguards outside the model, log every meaningful action as tamper-proof human-readable evidence, and guarantee an authorized human can pause or kill a model mid-task. Notably, this lands one day after Anthropic's report. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/10/microsofts-satya-nadella-says-ai-models-need-an-emergency-brake/)

**TypeSafe AI raised $870M at a $7.5B valuation, weeks after launching Jev.** a16z led, with Sequoia and DCVC. Jev is transformer-based but not an LLM — it outputs probabilities the company calls "calibrated decisions" rather than text, claiming much lower token cost for automation work. Founded 2024 by ex-OpenAI researcher Diogo Almeida, ex-Meta engineer Sasha Sheng, and Erik Gafni. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/09/the-maker-of-non-text-ai-model-jev-valued-at-7-5b-just-weeks-after-launch/)

[Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/9/deno-is-joining-cloudflare/)
