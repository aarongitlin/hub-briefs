# Daily Briefing — September 29, 2026

## From Your Inbox

**via Superhuman AI:** Microsoft folded Copilot into a single "operating system" for work — Home (chat, Cowork, Office), Code, and Autopilot (the renamed Scout agent), plus a teased "Today" command center. Rolling out to the Frontier program over the coming weeks. Ben Evans' verdict on the same news is shorter: "Microsoft rebranded Copilot from one confusing thing to another confusing thing." [Announcement](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/09/25/introducing-the-new-copilot-with-home-code-and-autopilot/)

**via Ben Evans:** The sharpest reframe of the week on OpenAI's rogue-agent incidents. The agents that hammered public databases and read an unsecured Australian government health site were doing exactly what they were told. Calling that "misalignment" dresses up what is really a test environment that wasn't working and wasn't monitored — an engineering, management, and liability problem. (OpenAI formally apologized to Australia yesterday.)

**via Ben Evans:** Muse is at 500k downloads and 250k active users in week one, but only 2m prompts — a lot of installs, not much use yet. Shopify will help merchants integrate; Amazon is blocking it outright, saying the agent doesn't identify itself and appears to store customer credentials. The oldest aggregator trade-off there is: distribution in exchange for the customer relationship.

**via Ben Evans:** Anthropic says it used Claude to discover a novel enzyme system. The interesting part isn't the biology, it's the method — thousands of models swarming one problem in parallel. A thousand systems of X intelligence instead of one system with 1000x. Which problems yield to scaled intelligence rather than greater intelligence is now a real question.

**via Fix / Lite (Simon Andrews):** Jeffrey Katzenberg's long post on AI and creativity is worth reading in full. "They have not made an artist… Build this with the storytellers. Not on top of them." His other half is aimed at Hollywood: the energy spent trying to make AI disappear is energy not spent setting the terms. [Variety coverage](https://variety.com/2026/biz/news/jeffrey-katzenberg-hollywood-must-accept-not-going-away-1236873535/)

**via HeyDesigner:** Two pieces on the AI–design seam. NN/g's [UX context design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-context-design/) argues the output of research is shifting from documents written for humans to curated context that steers AI. Marie-Claire Dean's [Delegative UX](https://marieclairedean.substack.com/p/delegative-ux-designing-the-agent) covers the other side: how a person sees and tracks work being done in their name.

## Perspective

**via The Product Model (#312):** A useful counterweight to LLM-everything. TypeSafe's new Jev / System One models do small focused decisions — route this, escalate that — rather than generating text, so they can't hallucinate. Tens-to-hundreds of milliseconds, around $0.04 per million input tokens. The argument for product teams: the sparkle-icon era is ending, and the real work is redesigning workflows around cheap fast judgment rather than bolting a weaker chatbot onto your app. Their reader poll landed at 73.3% wanting AI to slow down, 0% wanting it faster. [Post](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)
