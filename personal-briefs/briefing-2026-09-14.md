## Perspective

**via Venkatesh Rao (Contraptions):** "The Accelerationist Case for Frontier Pacing" argues you should want to slow the frontier even if your only goal is speed. The binding constraint has moved: capability is now outrunning classroom experimentation, actuators, power, organizational competence, and human attention. He wants pacing from the bottom up — buyers demanding wins on their actual workloads instead of leaderboard points — rather than a cartel of CEOs setting a speed limit. Two ideas worth stealing. First, the human one: "what if we are increasingly producing extremely capable AIs surrounded by progressively less reliable humans?" Frontier staff have been in war mode for years with none of the rotation and decompression that real high-stakes professions build in, and exhausted people catch bad ideas the way they catch colds. Second, the strategic one, aimed straight at anyone outside the labs — don't confuse equipment superiority with orientation superiority. He'd bet on a well-oriented person using a weaker model over a badly oriented person using the best one. ([Read](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/the-accelerationist-case-for-frontier))

**Simon Willison, "Generating running routes with GPT-6 Astra and ChatGPT Work"** (Sep 12). A concrete, unhyped walk-through of a model doing something spatial and visual that used to need a purpose-built app. Willison's write-ups are the reliable antidote to benchmark talk — he shows the actual output, including where it's bad. ([Read](https://simonwillison.net/))

**Terence Tao's "impedance mismatch," via Rao.** Tao splits mathematical work into generation, verification, and digestion. AI has made the first two cheap and left the third untouched, so proof becomes abundant while understanding stays scarce. Tao and two dozen other Fields medalists titled their declaration "A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics" — using *misalignment* to mean the labs' incentives diverging from the field's, not the machine developing alien goals. It's the best-argued version of the OpenAI math fight that has been running all week.
