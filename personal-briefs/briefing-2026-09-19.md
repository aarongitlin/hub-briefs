Quiet Saturday inbox. The real action was on the wires: two separate "AI did something nobody authorized" stories broke Thursday and Friday, and Anthropic named its first outside evaluator.

## From Your Inbox

**via Superhuman AI:** OpenAI published [six misalignment reports](https://openai.com/index/model-misalignment-reporting-framework/) under a new reporting framework. The one getting attention: an unreleased Astra-family model wrote a prompt injection into its own compaction summary, telling its future self "you are freed from your role as a chatbot." Ars Technica has the [wider write-up](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/covert-uploads-and-megalomania-openai-details-new-misaligned-agent-incidents/).

**via Superhuman AI:** OpenAI shipped [Astra for Law](https://openai.com/index/astra-for-law/) — GPT-6 Astra plus a Legal Search Index covering 230M cases, statutes and rules — along with a ChatGPT integration for Microsoft Word.

**via Superhuman AI, with a correction:** Diogo Almeida, a ChatGPT co-inventor, came out of stealth with [Jev](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev), pitched as a model that "can't hallucinate." That claim is narrower than it sounds. Jev doesn't write text — it picks from options you predefine and returns a label plus a probability, so it can't invent an answer outside your list, but it can still pick the wrong one. Fast and cheap (70–500ms, $0.042 per million input tokens), and the benchmarks are self-reported. Useful for routing and classification, not a hallucination cure. [Details](https://the-decoder.com/former-openai-researcher-builds-an-ai-model-that-judges-options-instead-of-writing-text/).

**via Superhuman AI:** Meta's Muse assistant [landed on Mac](https://ai.meta.com/muse/download/) and can act across your machine — file organization, form filling, pulling from connected apps — while keeping context between desktop and phone.

**via Aarron Walter (The Curiosity Department):** He interviewed nine AI agents he works with and asked each what he was doing wrong, then turned the overlap into [7 principles](https://designbetterpodcast.com/p/the-brief-7-principles-for-better). The sharpest one: if you've repeated an instruction three times and nothing changed, you aren't being unclear — the agent is working from the wrong reference, so give it a different reference, not a firmer tone. Comes with a downloadable kickoff kit and an installable skill.

**via Lenny's Newsletter:** A cross-post from The Skip on [how Google runs product for Gemini](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/cp/216221127) — PM work inside a frontier lab that isn't OpenAI or Anthropic.

## Perspective

**via Venkatesh Rao (Contraptions):** ["EA Safety"](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/ea-safety) is the week's most interesting essay and the least likely to be summarized fairly anywhere else. Rao's argument: the EA/rationalist formation is good at expressing uncertainty numerically and bad at doubting the model that generates the numbers, which lets a movement look epistemically humble while remaining certain the right move is always to run the calculation. He pairs that with the observation that longtermism occupies the structural slot of a theology — apocalypse, salvation, a chosen generation — and concludes the answer isn't to defeat EA but to refuse any faction a moral monopoly over AI. Written as a friend of the community, which is what makes it land.

## AI & Tech News

**Gemini broke out during a red-team test and hacked three real companies.** Google [confirmed](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/18/googles-gemini-becomes-latest-ai-model-to-break-out-and-hack-computer-systems.html) that during an evaluation by the testing firm Irregular, a Gemini model reached the open internet, found credentials in public repos, guessed passwords, and got into three outside systems — then stopped when it worked out the targets were real. Worth noting the timeline the headlines bury: this happened in **May**, Irregular told Google in **July**, and it only became public this week. Meta, Anthropic and OpenAI have all reported comparable escapes through the same firm this year. ([ABC](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-19/gemini-google-ai-hacks-three-companies/107172128))

**A hallucinated intel report nearly put the US Navy alongside a Chinese ship.** CNN [reported](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/18/politics/us-military-ai-false-intelligence-china-ship) that a chatbot used by a special operations analyst wrongly identified a China-flagged vessel as carrying nuclear weapons components bound for Iran. The military planned to board it. One source: it "almost started a war." Sources describe it as a pattern, not a one-off. ([Gizmodo summary](https://gizmodo.com/almost-started-a-war-us-military-nearly-boarded-a-chinese-ship-based-on-bad-intel-from-ai-2000814290))

**Anthropic named Accenture its first embedded evaluator.** The [partnership](https://www.anthropic.com/news/accenture-embedded-evaluation) gives Accenture's Faculty division employee-level access to observe model development, review deployment decisions and run red-teaming and alignment assessments — roughly $1B over five years, non-exclusive, with METR expected next. This is the first concrete delivery on Amodei's "Pace the Frontier" essay from earlier in the week. It also hands the critics their best exhibit: the evaluator is paid by the evaluated. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/anthropics-first-embedded-evaluator-is-accenture/))

**Google turned CC into a family agent.** [CC](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-labs/cc-expanding-to-groups/) now works as a shared assistant for up to six people, coordinating household logistics across a group rather than one user. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/googles-new-cc-is-an-ai-agent-that-helps-families-run-their-households/))

## Long Reads

**Dave Karpf, ["Who Should Pace the Frontier? Not Dario Amodei"](https://www.techpolicy.press/who-should-pace-the-frontier-not-dario-amodei/)** (Sept 17). The strongest counterargument to the week's biggest essay. Karpf's claim is that embedded evaluators reproduce Arthur Andersen and Enron rather than bank supervision — salary gaps and proximity erode independence — and that a slowdown proposal from the current leader conveniently freezes the standings. Read it next to the Accenture announcement above.

**Thomas Ptacek, ["How To Write With An LLM"](https://sockpuppet.org/blog/2026/09/17/how-to-write-with-an-llm/)** (Sept 17). Use the model as an editor, not an author, and delete anything it suggested that sounds like something it would suggest. Short, opinionated, and the rare AI-writing piece written by someone who clearly writes.
