**via Lenny's Newsletter (Elena Verna): "The end of feature discovery as we know it."** Verna argues that decades of onboarding tours, nudges and lifecycle emails mostly failed to get people to use features. AI makes all that moot: the user states a goal and the product decides which capability to use. The new growth problem is getting users to trust the product with the work, so reliability, transparency, control and undo become growth levers. Her warning for designers: an AI bolted on as a feature is just one more feature nobody discovers, and an empty prompt box still doesn't tell users what's possible. It's an opinion piece with no data behind it. [Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/cp/223665135)

## Perspective

**via Venkatesh Rao: "Descent into the Computational Sublime."** Rao calls October "Black October." That month AI-assisted researchers found a string of flaws in PHP, Linux networking and UEFI firmware, and attackers took about $6.34B from Binance on Oct 29. Part of that attack went through an internal AI tool. FBI attribution: North Korea. About $2.85B was frozen. His frame: AI is an "irresistible force," and cryptographic "hardness" is an "immovable object." Either one can serve attackers or defenders. The bottleneck is now patching, not finding bugs. It's long, but it explains the security news of the past month better than most reporting does. [Contraptions](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/descent-into-the-computational-sublime)

**via John Cutler: TBM 449, "Game Design, Consulting, and the Engagement as Product."** Cutler treats a consulting engagement as a cooperative game. It needs clear goals, a difficulty both sides can win at, visible progress, and milestones that cut risk rather than Gantt-chart "% complete." He names three traps: "we know what's best for you," "the customer is always right," and incentives nobody says out loud. His example of doing it right is Pivotal Labs, which sold its own way of working as part of the product. This applies to anyone designing how an internal team works with its stakeholders, not just to consultants. [The Beautiful Mess](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-449-game-design-consulting-and)

## Long Reads

**Stratechery: "AI on the Ballot."** Ben Thompson argues that AI's limiting factor has moved from compute, to memory and power, and now to politics: "the final constraint is political." Opposition to data centers isn't partisan, since both parties see it as a winning issue. So the industry can no longer assume it gets the benefit of the doubt, and "is going to need to do some convincing." Read it with the Rao piece: one is about what voters are refusing, the other about what the software underneath is failing at. [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2026/ai-on-the-ballot/)
