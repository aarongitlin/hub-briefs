# Daily Briefing — September 16, 2026

## From Your Inbox

**via Superhuman AI:** Diogo Almeida — ex-OpenAI, a co-inventor of RLHF — came out of two years of stealth with **Jev**, from his startup TypeSafe AI ($40M raised). The pitch is a "System One Model" that returns typed probabilistic decisions instead of prose: 70–500ms responses, $0.042/MTok input and $0 output, and no hallucinations. Read the claim precisely. Jev cannot generate text at all, so "hallucination-free" is close to a category statement rather than a benchmark result — The Register makes the same point, and nothing here is independently verified yet. Still the interesting shape of the week: a model that is deliberately not a chatbot. ([TypeSafe](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev), [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/16/typesafe-ai-debuts-model-for-machines-that-plays-doom/5296711))

**via Superhuman AI:** Anthropic is pressing toward a 2026 Nasdaq listing at a **$2T valuation** and told investors to expect a second straight profitable quarter. The caveat matters more than the headline: that profit figure excludes training costs and shared revenue. The company also told investors it sees a $30T revenue opportunity, roughly a quarter of world GDP, against about $65B annualized today. One footnote — Superhuman credits the IPO reporting to Bloomberg; the sourcing that's traceable runs back to the FT. ([Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/09/14/anthropic-tells-investors-it-will-be-profitable-for-second-straight-quarter/), [Morningstar on the caveat](https://www.morningstar.com/news/marketwatch/2026091414/the-very-big-caveat-to-the-report-that-anthropic-is-profitable-for-a-second-straight-quarter))

**via Superhuman AI:** Anthropic also shipped a **Salesforce plugin for Claude** with 37 sales-specific skills — same week Salesforce held its own agent event. The enterprise fight is moving from model quality to who owns the workflow surface. ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/salesforce-in-claude))

**via Superhuman AI:** Zach Yadegari (Cal AI) launched **Persona**, a wrist band you talk to by raising or tapping your arm. Agent learns your habits, replies by text, three-day battery. The wearable-agent form factor keeps getting re-attempted; nobody has yet shown the retention curve that would make it a category. ([Persona](https://yourpersona.com/band#top))

**via Superhuman AI:** The safety argument got a clean two-column framing this week — former Anthropic staffer Jacob Coxxon's "gambling with our lives" on one side, regulatory-capture skepticism on the other. Worth noting the structural point the skeptics make: the labs loudest about regulation are the ones best positioned to survive it.

**via Lenny's Newsletter:** Tom Orbach (growth at Wiz) published **64 creative growth tactics**, each one actually run by a real company. The framing is the useful part — treat them as lottery tickets, not a playbook, because each has been used once in one niche. Standouts: putting your logo where people screenshot, moving a daily product action somewhere public ("word-of-sight"), and Zoom's buried post-attendee URL setting pointed at your testimonials page. There's a Markdown version built for feeding to an agent. ([Read](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/60-creative-growth-ideas))

**via Rands in Repose:** Michael Lopp on giving the same talk for ten years. He wrote *The Second Act* in 2016, gives it once or twice a year, and improves it slightly each time — against his old instinct to build a new talk per event. Two lessons, both about compounding: build evergreen content rather than fresh content, and you never know which past relationship becomes the one that matters. The Chicago talk exists because of a roller hockey trip twenty years ago. ([Read](https://randsinrepose.com/))
