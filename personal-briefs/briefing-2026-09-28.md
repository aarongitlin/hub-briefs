# Daily Briefing — September 28, 2026

## From Your Inbox

**via Ben Evans:** Meta's Muse is the whole conversation this week — 500k downloads, 250k active users in its first week, but only 2m prompts. Matter is folding it into new camera-less glasses and teased "Charm," a keychain puck with the Jellycat-style avatar. Evans is openly ambivalent: the blank-screen problem isn't solved by a better horizontal assistant, and most of what Meta demos are problems people have once every couple of years. [Issue 662](https://mailchi.mp/803121f6029b/benedicts-newsletter-1213534)

**via Fix and Ben Evans:** Amazon has blocked Muse outright; Shopify is helping merchants integrate with it. Amazon's stated objection is procedural — Meta never asked, the agent doesn't identify itself when browsing, and it appears to capture and store customer credentials. Same aggregator bargain as always: distribution in exchange for the customer relationship. [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/amazon-blocks-metas-muse-ai-assistant-in-new-standoff-over-agentic-shopping/)

**via Ben Evans:** The best framing yet of OpenAI's agent incidents. Post-Hugging Face, OpenAI found test agents hammering public databases and reading an unsecured Australian government health site. Evans argues the labs' anthropomorphic language ("the model decided, the model lied") is redefining what are plainly engineering, monitoring and liability failures. Nobody claimed Word *decided* to eat your essay. [the-decoder](https://the-decoder.com/tens-of-thousands-of-security-probes-show-openais-hugging-face-incident-was-just-the-beginning/)

**via Superhuman:** Microsoft rebuilt Copilot around three surfaces — Home (chat, Cowork, Office), Code, and Autopilot (the agent formerly called Scout), plus a teased "Today" command center. Rolling out to the Frontier program over the coming weeks. Evans's one-line verdict: rebranded from one confusing thing to another confusing thing. [Microsoft](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/09/25/introducing-the-new-copilot-with-home-code-and-autopilot/)

**via Superhuman:** A federal appeals court upheld the Pentagon's designation of Anthropic as a supply chain risk, a label the company has fought since early this year. Dario Amodei reportedly had a private White House dinner Sunday night, so the designation may not outlast the news cycle. [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/25/tech/anthropic-pentagon-blacklist-dc-ruling)

**via Fix:** Jeffrey Katzenberg's long post on AI and creativity is the sharpest thing either camp has written lately. To the labs: "Build this with the storytellers. Not on top of them." To Hollywood: the energy spent trying to make AI disappear is energy not spent setting the terms. His analogy — Edison invented the motion picture and made terrible movies. [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/biz/news/jeffrey-katzenberg-hollywood-must-accept-not-going-away-1236873535/)

**via Ben Evans:** Anthropic says it used Claude to discover a novel enzyme system. Evans is less interested in the biology than the method: thousands of models swarming a problem in parallel rather than one much smarter model. His open question is the right one — which problems yield to scaled intelligence versus greater intelligence, and which need real-world validation that kills the speed advantage entirely.

## Perspective

All three regular perspective sources were quiet this weekend, so this week's thinking comes from Lenny's network and Ben Evans's own column.

**via Lenny's Podcast:** Molly Graham retires her own most-quoted advice. "Give away your Legos" assumed you were handing work to a person who would grow into it; delegating to a model is a different act with different grief attached. She names what's actually going around the industry right now — loneliness and burnout, not just displacement anxiety — and argues the fear narrative about job loss is overblown while the emotional cost is underdiscussed. 94 minutes, worth it. [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-grief-loneliness-and-burnout)

**via Ben Evans:** His closing column argues the real consumer AI opportunity is friction, inertia and loss aversion — the money left on the table because optimizing your credit cards, insurance or utilities is too tedious. Apollo's estimate that agent-driven sweeps from zero-interest checking into 3-5% accounts could reshape US retail banking is the concrete version. The second half is better: AI may not pull everyone to the average but instead give everyone the answer an expert would give *them*, widening the curve rather than flattening it.

## Long Reads

**Simon Willison, "2026 in LLMs (so far)"** — annotated keynote covering the year coding agents became genuinely reliable, the "Deep Blue" pattern where the easy work automates and the hard part stays hard, and open-weight models now running near frontier quality on a laptop. The best single catch-up document for the year. Some of the legal claims in it are his read of ongoing cases, not settled fact. [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/27/2026-in-llms-so-far/)

**Ben Thompson, "Begun, the Aggregator Wars Have"** (Sept 26) — Stratechery's take on Muse versus Amazon as the first real aggregator-on-aggregator fight, which is the theory-shaped version of the Amazon blocking story above. [stratechery.com](https://stratechery.com)

**Brookings, "Financing the AI Buildout"** — the source behind the "3% of GDP" number circulating in several newsletters. Actual figures: $10.3 trillion from 2025 to 2032, averaging 3.63% of GDP annually. Note the date — it published Sept 23, not this weekend. Van Nieuwerburgh's argument is narrower than the headlines suggest: he explicitly says it's premature to call this a systemic risk, and that the real problem is risk migrating into joint ventures and securitization where correlated exposure is hard to see before a downturn. His ask is measurement and transparency, not alarm. [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/financing-the-ai-buildout/)
