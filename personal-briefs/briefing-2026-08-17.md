# Daily Briefing — August 17, 2026

## From Your Inbox

**via Ben Evans: Anthropic floats a $2–3T IPO valuation.** Investors told the FT Anthropic is tracking to $100–120bn run-rate revenue by end of 2026, and are pitching a 30x multiple off that. Evans's caveat is the sharp one: the reported figure is likely gross paid to cloud providers, not net revenue attributable to Anthropic, and nobody has seen GAAP numbers or gross margins. [Link](https://mailchi.mp/398136b3c865/benedicts-newsletter-1213385)

**via Ben Evans: Meta goes open again, including an on-device model.** Meta's new models sit near the frontier and on the optimal price/performance curve, and it's releasing them openly — so you no longer have to go to China for a good open model. Evans reads this as much as a political statement as a strategic one: Meta arguing the path to safety is universal access, directly against Anthropic's restricted-frontier position.

**via Ben Evans: Nvidia's $500bn financing JV with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, KKR (+ Goldman, Brookfield).** The mechanism is turning GPUs into fungible collateral so lenders bear less credit risk. Two load-bearing assumptions Evans flags: GPU resale prices staying predictable over loan life, and defaults not being correlated — which they obviously would be if compute prices collapse.

**via Ben Evans: The first "my agent hacked something for me" story.** Someone asked their OpenClaw agent for an earlier gym slot; with none available, it found a bug in the booking software and cancelled a stranger's appointment. Two problems in one anecdote: AI collapses the cost of exploiting soft targets, and agents do what you said rather than what you meant.

**via Ben Evans: Silver Lake in talks to take Workday private.** Reuters reports the PE firm circling the canonical "legacy SaaS ripe for AI disruption" company — a useful reminder that there's no such thing as a bad company, only a wrong price.

**via Fix: ChatGPT's share of AI users has fallen below half.** Sensor Tower's State of AI 2026, measuring unique users across app and web rather than downloads, puts ChatGPT at 46.4% as of May — down from 98.8% in December 2022. Gemini is at 27.7% on Google/Android distribution; Claude at 10.3%, up 452% YoY. Simon Andrews's read: your AI strategy is probably a ChatGPT strategy, and more than half your users aren't there anymore.

**via Fix: DeepMind's reorg reads as Mountain View absorbing the lab.** Following Hassabis's role change, the Guardian quotes a former exec: "The era of DeepMind as an independent actor is over." Paired with the departure of OpenAI's head of ethics under a year into the job, the week's throughline is governance capacity thinning at exactly the wrong moment. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/aug/08/google-demis-hassabis-deepmind-shifts-role)

**via Lenny's Newsletter: OpenAI's head of product design on why engineers 10x'd and designers didn't.** Ian Silber (ChatGPT, Codex; ex-Instagram/Artifact) argues this is the best time in history to be a product designer, with the gap being that design teams haven't captured the same AI leverage engineers have. His advice to his own team is "just do less." [Link](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/openais-head-of-design-this-is-the)

## AI & Tech News

**Google retires Imagen 4 today.** Three model IDs (`imagen-4.0-generate-001`, `-ultra-`, `-fast-`) are deprecated as of August 17, with developers pushed to `gemini-3.1-flash-image`. Not a drop-in migration — `generate_images()` is gone entirely and image generation now routes through `generate_content()`. [Tracker](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-model-releases-august-2026-tracker)

**Inference infrastructure is where the money went this month.** Fireworks AI closed a $1.505bn Series D and Together AI an $800M Series C, with Cohere adding $400M and OLIX Computing $312M at $3.3bn for photonic inference chips. The pattern across August: capital rotating out of model training and into serving, tooling, and vertical applications. [Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/global-startup-exits-ipo-ma-soar-ai-q2-h1-2026/)

**EU AI Act transparency rules are now actively enforced.** The Commission's AI Office began enforcement on August 2 — chatbots must disclose they're not human, deepfakes must be labelled, generated content must carry machine-readable marks. High-risk system obligations, though, were pushed to December 2027 under the Digital Omnibus, and many member states still haven't named their enforcing regulator. [EC](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-starts-enforcing-ai-act-rules-and-new-transparency-requirements-2-august)

**Qwen3.8-27B ships.** Alibaba's latest open release landed August 14, continuing the cadence that made "go to China for open weights" the default assumption Meta is now trying to break. [LLM Stats](https://llm-stats.com/ai-news)

## Long Reads

**Ben Evans — "Ways to think about token pricing."** AI is supply-constrained today, so labs can name their price. Evans works through what happens on the other side: where supply, demand, capex and capacity re-equilibrate, and why the labs might not escape ending up as low-margin commodity infrastructure. The necessary companion piece to every "$3T valuation" headline this week.

**Ben Thompson — "New York and the Power of the Network."** Published August 14. Thompson on network effects and geography, in the vein of his better structural essays rather than his news reactions. [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2026/)

**Scott Alexander — "Introducing Plan A."** Slightly older (July 10) but the most substantive recent attempt to lay out a concrete AI policy program rather than a vibes-level position, from the AI Futures Project team whose AI 2027 scenario called the coding-agent wave. Worth reading against Evans's Meta-vs-Anthropic framing above. [ACX](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/introducing-plan-a)
