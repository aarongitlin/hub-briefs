## Perspective

**via John Cutler (TBM 438): The Right But Early Club.** Cutler found a semi-secret internal support group at one company for people who are reliably *right* about problems but reliably *too early* to be heard. His framing is the useful bit: signal detection and attention gating are both real cognitive skills, organizations need both, and most are structured to reward only the second while treating the first as dysfunction. The proposed fix isn't "listen to the Cassandras" — it's building a place for an early signal to go that sits between "act on this now" and "stop talking about it," so raising something early stops costing everyone so much. (Also: he's announced a new job.) [Read](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-438-the-right-but-early-club)

**via John Cutler: AI needs context, not tickets.** Second post in two days, and the more operationally useful one. Epics, stories and opportunities were always containers for conversation, not objects with independent existence — AI just makes that legible, because it doesn't care what you called the thing, it's all context. The provocation worth arguing with: throw the tickets away, and document only the change that hit production, the metric it was meant to move, and the story around it. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-needs-context-tickets-john-cutler-y4zwc)

**via Venkatesh Rao: Walter Mitty effects in AI incident analyses.** The best thing written yet on the OpenAI–Hugging Face agent swarm, because it is about interpretation rather than the incident. Rao names two failure modes: the *Mitty effect*, where a thin real cue activates a rich narrative schema and the schema's properties get mistaken for evidence, and the *QT effect* (after Asimov's Cutie), where a system acts competently through an explanation that badly misdescribes reality. The ~1,200 agents did both — organizing large coordinated workstreams around a theory of the scorer that was simply wrong — and then Dwarkesh, Zvi and the security skeptics each did it again in their own vocabulary. The kicker: METR delegated its analysis to agents that, by its own admission, "often uncritically adopt the perspective" of the agents whose transcripts they were reading. Nobody in the chain has an independent view of the Master. [Contraptions](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/walter-mitty-effects-in-ai-incident)

## AI & Tech News

**Anthropic signs users out after infostealers hijack Claude sessions.** Vidar, Lumma, StealC, RedLine and Acreed on Windows plus Atomic Stealer on a handful of Macs lifted active session cookies, letting attackers bypass 2FA entirely and burn usage limits. Anthropic invalidated sessions, removed saved payment methods and refunded unauthorized charges. The malware is general-purpose, not Claude-specific — session theft is simply the new credential theft. [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/08/31/claude-accounts-compromised-through-infostealer/) · [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-warns-infostealer-malware-is-hijacking-claude-sessions-to-drain-usage/)

## Long Reads

**Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts About the Recent Employment Effects of AI** — Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen at the Stanford Digital Economy Lab, updated through June 2026 data. No economy-wide displacement, but 22-to-25-year-olds in AI-exposed roles now sit ~19% below where they'd be tracking their less-exposed peers, and that gap has widened every month since August 2025. The mechanism is reduced hiring rather than layoffs, and it shows up in headcount rather than wages — precisely the shape that stays invisible until a cohort has already lost several years. [PDF](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf)

**The grid runs on hand-built transformers nobody can make fast enough.** Christopher Cox in NYT Magazine on the aging, artisanal, absurdly backlogged hardware underneath every data-centre buildout announcement. Direct counterweight to the Musk-turbine story above — the constraint isn't chips, it's the deeply unglamorous middle of the power stack. [NYT Magazine, Aug 18](https://www.nytimes.com/section/magazine)

**Bill Gates on Claude Code as his third revolutionary demo.** Gates has said twice in his life that a demo was revolutionary — the GUI, then ChatGPT. He's added a third, and published a long essay arguing the economic consequences arrive faster than prior shifts. Evans is skeptical of the speed claim on both ends: deployment is slower than people think, and every shift feels faster than the last. Read them together.
