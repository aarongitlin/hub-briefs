## Perspective

**via The Beautiful Mess (John Cutler):** [Ten years on from "You Are Working in a Feature Factory"](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-445-the-feature-factory-a-10), Cutler mostly argues against his younger self. The post went viral because it was punchy and judgmental, which he admits was partly the point of writing it. His retrospective: the outcomes-vs-outputs binary was always false, the projects-to-products wave caused real damage by over-hiring PMs and setting design back, and a well-run feature factory beats a team that can discover but can't ship. The sharpest line is about AI — it "amplifies the bad habits and creates leverage for the good ones," with teams now generating reasonable-sounding PRDs that eliminate the useful back-and-forth, and synthetic personas standing in for customers nobody was talking to anyway.

## Long Reads

**[Neural Networks Are Already Clean](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/)** — Nick Cammarata (Goodfire, ex-OpenAI) argues interpretability has been chasing the wrong problem. The famous polysemantic neurons were misread: the cat-and-car neuron is really a V-shape detector, and apparent mixtures come from weak tools and bad architecture, not superposition. His evidence includes retraining InceptionV1 with modern changes, which made the last layer far more interpretable, and an audit of Qwen3-32B where he estimates ~90% of neurons have one main job. He calls it a working hypothesis, not a result. If it holds, inspecting a model before release gets much cheaper.

**[A Conversation With Ari Emanuel About Entertainment and AI](https://stratechery.com/)** — Ben Thompson and the Hollywood super-agent on his memoir, what AI does to the industry's economics, and the enduring value of a single phone call. Thompson is unusually good at getting operators to explain their actual leverage.

**[Why AI won't kill the creative class](https://www.ft.com/content/f17779e4-adcd-46af-9fe5-3ede05833058)** — Richard Florida returns to his own thesis, arguing the technology can unleash human creativity rather than replace it. Useful as the counterweight to the hollowing-out argument, though Florida has a track record of defending the creative class against whatever is threatening it this decade.
