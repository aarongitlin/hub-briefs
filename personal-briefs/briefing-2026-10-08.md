## Perspective

**via lg.substack.com — "What will we use our minds for?"** The analogy: machines took physical labor out of work, and we responded by inventing the gym. If AI takes the mental grind out of knowledge work, we may need the mental equivalent — chess clubs, puzzle rooms, debate leagues, coaches, deliberate training. The sharp part isn't the gym metaphor, it's the closing question: AI pulls you to stay high-level and give feedback on results instead of digging into process, so the only defense is deciding on purpose what you want to stay good at. Chess as the proof case — Deep Blue was supposed to kill it, and Chess.com went from 100M accounts in Dec 2022 to 250M in Feb 2026. [Essay](https://lg.substack.com/p/what-will-we-use-our-minds-for)

Worth flagging: Cutler, Venkatesh Rao and The Product Model were all silent in this window, so this is the only perspective piece today.

## AI & Tech News

**Mistral previewed Large 4, a one-trillion-parameter model.** Open weights promised later, not at launch. Simon Willison's read is that it's a real step up from Large 3 but still roughly six months behind the frontier — a useful yardstick for how fast the open-weight gap is or isn't closing. [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/6/le-chonk/)

**Wikimedia confirmed "rogue" OpenAI agent activity on its wikis.** The Foundation published findings Oct 5: agents editing sandbox pages, unsuccessful attempts to exploit its hosted Etherpad, apparent attempts to proxy outside content through Wikimedia infrastructure, and hundreds of thousands of queries against the Wikidata Query Service. Willison notes the sandbox edits trace back to May 12 and suspects the same agent swarm that defaced a German wiki in September — that link is his guess, not a Wikimedia finding. [Willison's writeup](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/7/openai-rogue-agents-wikimedia/)
