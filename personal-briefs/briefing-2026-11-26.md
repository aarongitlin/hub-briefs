# Daily Briefing — November 26, 2026

## From Your Inbox

**via Superhuman: Epochal's "Time Portal" puts AI agents inside the internet of any past date.** Epochal, a startup from former xAI people, built a point-in-time copy of the web covering 30+ years and more than 1 trillion pages. Agents get "locked" on a date and asked to forecast what came next, which gives you a backtest for AI prediction. The launch post drew 3.3M views. Superhuman calls the results "surprisingly robust," but I couldn't read Epochal's own post to check the evidence, so treat the accuracy claims as unverified for now. [Epochal](https://epochal.com/blog/introducing-time-portal)

**via Superhuman: The FCC approves SpaceX's plan for up to 1M AI data-center satellites.** The network is called Starmind. It cleared over objections from Amazon, Viasat and astronomers worried about light pollution. First launches are targeted for 2027, and the deadline to finish is 2040. Anthropic and Google are named as possible customers. SpaceX filed in February, so this is the final approval, not a new plan. [Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/fcc-approves-spacex-to-launch-1-million-orbital-data-centers/)

**via Superhuman: Nothing teases Ear (note), earbuds that take meeting notes.** The buds record calls. Mics in the charging case capture in-person conversations even while the buds stay in the case. An app turns it all into transcripts and summaries. [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/gadgets/1042162/nothing-ear-note-ai-earbuds-reveal)

**via Superhuman: "Effort signaling" is the holiday ad trend.** After last year's backlash against Coca-Cola's AI-made spot, brands are making a show of human craft. Geico used stop-motion for its Macy's Parade ad, Levi's used claymation, and Prada hired a former Disney animator. The pattern: AI as the finished product reads as lazy, while AI as a tool inside something human still works. Geico's AI Gecko on a live podcast with Azzi Fudd went over well. [Superhuman](https://www.superhuman.ai/p/new-startup-unveils-time-machine-for-ai)

**via Rands: "Three Lies."** Lopp's fourth original book is out. The post covers the three lies he tells about how long a book takes: "a few months," "a month to edit," "two years." The real number is about three years between books. It's a short, honest note on estimating creative work that you'll recognize from software. [Rands in Repose](https://news.randsinrepose.com/r/2327f9a1?m=60d9e261-36b3-4aec-9966-29bb285b519d)

## Perspective

**via John Cutler: "How to Get Unstuck Without Losing Yourself (or Pretending Everything Is Fine)."** This is his long Thanksgiving-week post. Cutler argues that career advice takes pieces of Stoicism, Buddhism and other traditions and drops the parts that ask for justice or collective action. The result turns trouble with one institution into a crisis about who you are. He offers five "modes" in place of a self-improvement ladder: the ground gives way, going in circles, engaging the tension, resting in the familiar, and riding a wave of coherence. Every mode has risks, and none of them is the goal. The best line: Munch's performance review, which suggests "greater emotional resilience" than his two colleagues showed. "Sometimes getting unstuck is itself an act of defiance." [The Beautiful Mess](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-453-how-to-get-unstuck-without)
