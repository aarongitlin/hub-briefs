**via Design Better: what three years of AI-for-designers workshops taught them.** Bobby Hughes looks back on running AI x design thinking workshops since 2024. Early on, AI worked best as a thought partner, so they built a "4 S's" framework: simplify, spark, shape, sharpen. By 2025 designers wanted to build, and kept getting stuck on basics like "what is a repo?" and "do I need an MCP server?" The gap between early adopters and everyone else is widening. The more interesting turn is toward "cognitive surrender": they cite a 666-person study (Gerlich, 2025) where frequent AI users scored lower on critical thinking, with cognitive offloading as the cause. Their question now is "how much AI, and when," which they test in a "Humans vs. AI" format where teams split on the same brief. ## Long Reads

**PPC Land, "Google, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic back one AI training opt-out" (Oct 31, ~22 min).** A detailed look at a new shared robots.txt token, `__usage_ai-training`, that lets a site opt out of AI training with one rule instead of one rule per crawler. Anthropic's Kevin Kelley submitted the IETF draft (draft-kelley-aipref-training-preference-signal-00), and Google, Microsoft, and OpenAI said Oct 30 they'll honor it. Microsoft starts Feb 1, 2027. Google starts "early 2027" and will treat the token like a Google-Extended disallow, which also blocks Gemini grounding. OpenAI and Anthropic gave no dates. Wildcard `User-agent: *` rules don't count, and Meta, Amazon, and Apple aren't on board. It's a rare cross-lab agreement on publisher consent, and the fine print is the point. [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-microsoft-openai-and-anthropic-back-one-ai-training-opt-out/)
