## From Your Inbox

**via Ben Evans:** Anthropic's IPO is shaping up as the big test of the AI capex cycle. Reported gross revenue was $16.8bn in Q3, up from $11.5bn in Q2 and $4.8bn in Q1 (versus $4.85bn for all of 2025), with a claimed operating profit before stock comp — but slowing growth may pull the target valuation from $2tr back toward $1.5tr. His wider point: capex has outgrown hyperscaler cash flow, so Wall Street now decides how far this goes, and the neocloud "chips are gold-plated collateral" argument fails if the whole segment reprices. He also notes the circularity: if lenders stop funding because they fear a glut, the glut never arrives.

**via Ben Evans (column, "Leverage"):** A useful frame for "what will AI do to industry X?" The internet remade some industries (music, retail), barely touched others (mining), and in the middle — airlines, banks — changed go-to-market without changing the core business. Two questions decide which bucket you're in: how far can the tech actually push into the problem, and does it hit the real point of leverage? His example: legal databases made precedent search 100x faster, which mattered little because precedent search was a tiny share of billable hours — a warning for "AI-native law firms."

## AI & Tech News

**Anthropic is putting 5% of its shares into a "public benefit fund."** It will be a wholly owned entity, with the option to add 2% more for every 50% rise in the company's value. Possible uses range from large gifts to established nonprofits to direct payments to affected groups. Who decides how it is spent is still open, and Anthropic says governance details will come in the coming months. It lands ahead of the IPO and amid what Dario Amodei has called a crisis of trust in AI. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/18/anthropic-says-its-committing-5-of-its-shares-to-a-public-benefit-fund/)

**Disney is taking most of Character.AI's staff out of bankruptcy.** Offers went to at least 60 employees, worth about $60m a year in total pay, and most were accepted. It is structured as a stalking-horse bid, so a higher offer could still win. This follows Disney's earlier $20m asset deal and the former Character.AI CEO becoming Disney's first CTO. The company's filing cited costs plus litigation and regulatory exposure over child safety. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/18/disney-agrees-to-hire-the-majority-of-character-ais-workforce/)

## Long Reads

**Ben Thompson, "Do You Trust Apple?"** The counterpoint to Evans on HomeView. Thompson thinks Apple could do for the smart home what AirPort did for Wi-Fi in 1999, and he lists the flaky thermostats, doorbells, and cameras in his own house as proof the pain is real. The catch is trust: Siri is still slower and less reliable after a two-year delay, and Apple has abandoned home hardware before (AirPort itself). Buying in means betting your home on Apple's AI. [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2026/do-you-trust-apple/)

**Daniel Bashir, "The Dream of an Aristotelian Machine."** How alignment drifted from rules and reward functions toward virtue ethics — RLHF, Constitutional AI, and character training, with Amanda Askell and Joe Carlsmith among the people betting that cultivated character generalizes better than rules. The sharp part is the closing worry: virtue is hard to verify from outside, and a model with real character might hold its values against its makers. That raises the question of whether we want that, and which values should be the fixed ones. [Asterisk](https://asteriskmag.com/issues/16/the-dream-of-an-aristotelian-machine)
