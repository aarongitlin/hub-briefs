## AI & Tech News

**A mathematician says he proved the Riemann hypothesis with Claude, and the Lean proof compiles.** Levent Alpöge says he worked with Claude to find a proof of the 167-year-old problem that extends to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, written in Lean (a language that lets a computer check every step of a proof). Independent checkers ran it, and MIT's Andrew Sutherland says it compiles. Mathematicians still have to understand the argument and confirm it states the right theorem, so "compiles" is not yet "accepted." The same day, Stanford's Ravi Vakil posted a separate GRH draft by postdoc Alessandro Masullo, 12 years of work, "all human, with no AI," and not yet in Lean. Alpöge also released a Navier–Stokes-related proof with NYU's Tristan Buckmaster. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/ai-claimed-a-solution-to-the-167-year-old-riemann-hypothesis-and-a-human-did-too/) · [Simon Willison on Alpöge](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/20/levent-alpoge/) · [Simon Willison on Vakil](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/20/ravi-vakil/)

**SpaceX's chip lenders reportedly don't want GPUs going to orbit.** The FT says lenders behind SpaceX's $40B chip financing asked that the chips not be used for its orbital data center plans. It's unclear whether SpaceX agreed. The worry is resale value: some H100s now sell for about half their 2023 cost, and a chip in orbit can't be repossessed. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/chips-can-survive-the-space-environment-can-their-financing/)
