## Perspective

**via Julie Zhuo (The Looking Glass): what will we use our minds for?** Zhuo runs the physical-labor analogy all the way through. Jobs requiring moderate physical activity fell from 48% in 1960 to 20% in 2008, and we responded by inventing gyms, trainers, Zone 2, and 1.3 million applicants for the 2027 London Marathon. If AI takes the cognitive load the way machines took the physical load, the same thing may happen to thinking: chess.com went from 100M accounts in Dec 2022 to 250M in Feb 2026, decades after Deep Blue. Her sharpest point is the paradox she heard from Alpha School's Joe Liemandt — the same AI that sharpens kids' brains in the morning block becomes a shortcut around effort in the afternoon. So the question is not whether to use the tool but what you actually want to get good at. [Read it](https://lg.substack.com/p/what-will-we-use-our-minds-for)

## AI & Tech News

**OpenAI's 719 math manuscripts are not clearing the field's bar.** Superhuman called it a "knowledge bomb"; the mathematicians disagree. Terence Tao: "Problems are being solved autonomously by AI prompters who have no interest in the broader field itself." Only 10 of the 719 included the model's chain of thought, and a Cambridge/King's paper found at least two discrepancies between OpenAI's natural-language proof and its Lean code on a Navier-Stokes-derived problem. OpenAI's own advisory group, AGMAI, says the release missed its guidelines on human understanding. Treat the original claim as unverified. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/openais-math-solutions-arent-meeting-the-fields-standards-yet/)
