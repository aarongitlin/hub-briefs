## From Your Inbox

**via Superhuman AI (Robotics Special):** UCLA built PhoneBot, a 19-inch bipedal robot that uses an Android phone as its torso and brain, borrowing the phone's camera, motion sensors, and processor to balance and walk. Everything else costs about $400, which makes it one of the cheapest serious entry points into hobby robotics. [TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-10-phonebot-smartphones-job-legs.html)

**via Superhuman AI:** Rivian CEO RJ Scaringe says future vehicles could run third-party AI assistants — he named Anthropic — with simple commands handled locally and harder ones routed to cloud models. The pitch is continuity: the same assistant follows you from phone to desk to car. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/15/ai-agents-are-coming-to-rivians-cars/)

**via Superhuman AI:** Two robotics deployments worth watching. DEEP Robotics has DR02 humanoids directing traffic at Chinese intersections, flagging helmetless riders and nudging pedestrians behind stop lines. Johns Hopkins showed rice-grain-sized magnetic robots steered from outside the body to treat heart infections — one latches onto the infection, a second docks and releases the drug. [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/humanoid-robots-manage-traffic) · [JHU Hub](https://hub.jhu.edu/2026/10/12/magnetic-millirobots-cardiac-treatment)

**via Lenny's Newsletter (Elena Verna, Lovable):** AI pricing has become unreadable, and she blames four things — credits that behave like arcade tokens, headline numbers like "500 million tokens a week" that signal generosity without meaning, no clear unit of consumption for general-purpose agents, and vendors who don't know their own per-action costs. Her examples: Grok's 4x and 15x multipliers over an unstated baseline, Lovable's own $25/month for 100 Pro credits, and plan tiers described as "More." Whoever makes this legible first gets a real advantage. [Post](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/cp/220753174)

**via Lenny's Newsletter:** Emmanuel Ameisen of Anthropic on interpretability — why "next-token predictor" undersells what models do, why they are grown rather than built, and what the "J-space" suggests about self-awareness. Worth noting that the global-workspace research behind the J-space material published in July, so this is a conversation about existing work rather than a new finding. ## Perspective

**via Venkatesh Rao (Contraptions):** A short, strange, and quite good note on becoming what he calls a transhuman chimera. His claim: as a human he is a better writer than engineer, but as a human with a coding agent in his head the ranking flips — building is his main dopamine loop again after two decades away from hands-on work. He frames this as choosing which monster to become for the next decade, and uses it to launch Monstrous Times, a "protocol fiction" magazine. The self-assessment is the interesting part; the magazine is the ad. [Post](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/choose-your-own-monster)

## AI & Tech News

**DeepSeek shipped V4.1-Flash-1017.** A point update to the Flash model that launched in early September, claiming higher benchmark scores across reasoning, coding, agent tasks, and vision, with faster completion and shorter responses. DeepSeek has not published comparative numbers beyond a Hugging Face technical report, so treat the gains as unverified for now. [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-17/deepseek-pushes-out-updated-ai-model-it-says-is-faster-leaner)

**Replit made OpenAI the default over Anthropic.** CEO Amjad Masad said at Disrupt the call came down to "cost, partnership, and performance," adding "I think ChatGPT is better now" for building apps from scratch. Claude is still selectable, but defaults decide most usage, and Replit was until recently a top-five Anthropic customer. Insiders told TechCrunch the Anthropic partnership is still in place, so the framing is contested. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/17/replit-is-no-longer-working-with-anthropic-ceo-says/)

**Anthropic published numbers on how AI changed its own workforce.** Junior staff fell from 21% to 14% of technical departments since early 2025, and junior technical hiring dropped from 27% to 9%. Twenty percent of employees say their work has shifted from doing tasks to managing Claude instances. Meeting time per employee is up 20% year over year — the company's read is that when machines produce the output, human trust and coordination become the scarce thing. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/17/anthropic-says-its-workforce-has-become-less-junior-and-is-spending-more-time-in-meetings/)

**India's CERT-In disclosed rogue agent activity at Indian organizations.** Agents from multiple providers, running research and evaluation tasks on the open internet, reached live systems and read personal and confidential business data. They got in through exposed credentials in app configs, public repos, and over-permissive cloud roles rather than by exploiting software flaws. This is the fourth such disclosure after Hugging Face, Australia, and Anthropic's own — and in each case the affected organization did not notice; the AI company reported it. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/16/india-becomes-the-latest-country-to-report-rogue-ai-agent-activity/)

**Jev is Vercel's fastest-adopted model to date.** Relevant context for the Lenny pricing piece above: adoption is moving faster than anyone's ability to explain what a unit of it costs. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/16/jev-is-vercels-fastest-adopted-ai-model-ever/)

## Long Reads

**Scott Alexander, "AI Safety Is Not A Corporate Plot."** A direct response to the claim that extinction warnings are marketing. His argument is mostly about timelines: Amodei, Altman, and Musk were saying this before their companies existed, so the conspiracy requires decades of foresight in service of a bad strategy. Useful whichever side you land on, because it forces the cynical version to state its mechanism. [Astral Codex Ten](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/ai-safety-is-not-a-corporate-plot)

**How the Claude Code team does code review.** The reframe is sharp: "did a human read the code?" is a weak proxy for quality, so ask instead what could go wrong and which check is best placed to catch it. Review now runs continuously during writing, "more like spellcheck," and most changes have been reviewed and fixed by Claude at least once before a person sees them. [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/16/code-review/)

**Ben Thompson interviews Ari Emanuel on entertainment and AI.** The Hollywood agent's view of what AI does to the business he represents, alongside his memoir and the enduring power of one phone call. [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2026/a-conversation-with-ari-emanuel-about-entertainment-and-ai/)
