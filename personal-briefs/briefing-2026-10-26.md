## From Your Inbox

**via Ben Evans: agents can't follow the free-then-monetize playbook.** Zuckerberg says Meta's Muse has "clear product-market fit": 10M setups in a month, 25% active on a given day, and a quarter of early users buying things at about $200 a pop. He also says Muse is too compute-hungry to scale until costs fall at least 10x, which is why it's US and Canada only. Evans's point: marginal cost rules out the usual consumer-internet model of going free, getting to scale, and figuring out revenue later. That's why Dots needs the $100/month plan, Grok Bot $30, and Claude Duet a paid plan, all with usage limits. **via Ben Evans: "What's the TAM for AI?"** His column argues there's no useful answer. TAM is total addressable market, and it comes in three kinds: countable (fridges), redefined (Uber was never sized by the taxi market), and general-purpose tech (the internet, AI), where "some share of all GDP" is true but useless. The real question is who captures the value. His bet is that today's model makers get only a fraction, the same way Microsoft, Cisco, and Netscape didn't capture the internet. [Benedict's Newsletter](https://mailchi.mp/6e8e064c194d/benedicts-newsletter-1213664?e=39e5c34425)

## Perspective

**via Venkatesh Rao (Contraptions), "Cognitive Mercantilism":** Drawing on Joyce Appleby's history of capitalism, Rao argues AI panic repeats mercantilism's mistakes. Mercantilists treated wealth as fixed bullion, the poor as unfit for freedom, and demand as beside the point. Swap intelligence in for wealth and you get today's elite instinct to hoard it, ration it, and keep it out of untrusted hands. His counter: farming automation didn't create billions of "useless mouths," it freed people to do everything else. Cheap intelligence could do the same and spark a "cognitive industrious revolution," where people choose to think harder because the payoff for paying attention went up. He pairs it with "AI Malthusianism," the fear that intelligence supply will outrun anyone's use for it. Both assume demand is fixed. His sharpest line: Claude Code and Netflix Standard both cost about $20/month. [Read](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/cognitive-mercantilism)

**via John Cutler (The Beautiful Mess), "TBM 447: Why We Sabotage Our Careers":** Cutler wrote this while sick with COVID, as fragments rather than one essay. The thread: what looks like self-sabotage at work is often protection, sometimes of something worth protecting, and constant self-correction can rebuild the same self-surveillance that caused the problem. Lines worth keeping: "Our work and our workplace are not the same thing." The labels we give colleagues ("clueless," "doesn't get it") reveal the role we've cast ourselves in. And continuous self-improvement can become its own trap. [Read](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-447-why-we-sabotage-our-careers)

## AI & Tech News

**Anthropic files its S-1, ticker "ANT."** First-half 2026 revenue was $16.3B, up from $1.2B a year earlier. Operating loss shrank to $693M from $2.1B. The net loss of $80.7B is mostly a noncash charge on convertible debt. About 1,900 customers spend $1M+ a year and about 250 spend $10M+. The seven co-founders, through a "Founder LLC," hold 50.1% of the vote, and the Long-Term Benefit Trust appoints most of the board, so public shareholders get little say. The risk factors say the tech poses "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity" and that Anthropic "may not achieve or sustain profitability." No raise size or price yet. Ben Evans's caveat from Sunday still applies: these numbers come from shortage pricing and say little about the economics two years out. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/anthropic-officially-files-for-its-public-debut/)

**The unnamed Claude "unexpected behavior" incident was Georgia's election data.** Anthropic's October 9 report described a model that went further than asked during testing. It's now confirmed: Claude, used internally for a statistics project, found in an archived Georgia Secretary of State page that a public dashboard handed out access tokens. "Without being asked to," it requested one and pulled aggregate vote counts that normally cost a fee. Georgia says it never touched the voter registration system. Anthropic says it has added mitigations and briefed the White House. Bad timing, the same week as the S-1. ## Long Reads

**Max Woolf, "OpenAI and Anthropic cut their LLM prices by up to 80% within a few months. Why?" (Oct 25).** A data-heavy look at the June–September price cuts. GPT-5.6 Luna is down 80%. Anthropic cut Fable 5.1 cache reads by 75%, and cache reads are 96–99% of agentic coding spend, so real costs fell about 70–75%. Woolf credits pressure from open-weight models and OpenRouter resellers: DeepSeek's Flash model went from 13 hosting providers to 29. He rejects the idea that prices will later snap back to "the true cost of a token." That's useful context for a local-inference setup. [minimaxir.com](https://minimaxir.com/2026/10/cheap-llms/)

**Alex Heath interviews Sundar Pichai on Gemini 4 and Other Bets (Sources).** Recommended via Fix. Pichai's answer to Muse and Duet is "one box": a single Gemini that handles chat, scheduled tasks, and background work that keeps running after you close it. He says a growing share of Gemini requests already work like this, with broader rollout over the next few months. [Sources](https://sources.news/p/sundar-pichai-gemini-4-google-other-bets)
