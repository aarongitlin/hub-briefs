# Weekly Brief — Nov 2–8, 2026

Amazon is building its own shopping agent while it blocks everyone else's, and the outside agents are still barely buying anything. On the design side, the theme was trust: the products that win will be the ones people hand work to, and teams are discovering what they lose when the slow, shared parts of the process disappear.

## Commerce × Media × AI

- **New since last week: Uber's agent numbers are even smaller than DoorDash's.** Uber's CFO said Halloween week brought about 320M trips and orders, and third-party agents placed about 500 Eats orders and 4 rides. Shopify, though, says orders from AI channels grew 3.3x in Q3 and convert 64% better than organic search. The volume is moving through assistant discovery and handoff to checkout, not through autonomous agent purchases. [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/only-500-uber-eats-orders-and-4-uber-rides-were-booked-by-ai-agents-on-halloween-weekend-says-uber-cfo/) · [Constellation](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/shopify-personal-ai-agent-traffic-starting-surge)
- **Amazon's answer to Muse is its own agent.** Amazon blocked Meta's Muse in September. This week it began testing a text-message Alexa agent that searches, buys, tracks prices and manages lists, and says it will reach "millions of users in coming weeks." This is the walled-garden version of agentic shopping, and its ad load stays under Amazon's control. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/after-blocking-muse-amazon-now-lets-users-text-alexa-to-help-them-shop/)
- **ChatGPT ads earn about a third of what Google search ads earn per visit.** A working paper puts it at $0.89 per session against $2.45, with 0.37% conversion against 1.17%. Adthena counts 6,333 brands advertising anyway. Shopping's share of US ChatGPT ad spend fell from 38% in April to 20% in September. Ads inside conversations reach users, but nobody has shown they convert. Caveat: the paper is not yet peer reviewed, and a co-author advises the data vendor. [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/in-graphic-detail-chatgpt-s-ad-growth-is-outrunning-the-proof-that-it-works/) · [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/ai/chatgpt-ads-vs-google-search-revenue-per-visit-study/)

## UX Practice × Agentic AI

- **The slow parts of the process were doing real work.** In TBM 450, John Cutler argues that "the bottleneck has moved" is too simple. Pairing, rounds of doc revision and "is this worth it?" questions were enabling constraints. Single-player agent workflows skip them, so teams now have to put that friction back on purpose. Design critique is exactly this kind of friction. [The Beautiful Mess](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-450-the-loss-of-positive-friction)
- **Feature discovery is dead, and trust is the new growth lever.** Elena Verna argues that tours and nudges never worked ("maybe 0.1%" of users find new features). AI makes them moot because the product picks the capability. Reliability, transparency and undo become what drives adoption. It's opinion with no data, but it gives design a clear job. [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/cp/223665135)
- **Ask for permission too often and the agent feels like something to supervise.** In Claire Vo's nine-task benchmark, Claude Duet asked for approval four times and every other agent asked once. That alone made it feel like an assistant she had to babysit, despite having some of the strongest coding judgment. How often to ask is a design decision, not a safety default. [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-i-ai-grok-vs-dot-vs-muse-vs-duet)
- **Apple now lists agents as a formal business risk.** Its FY2026 10-K names agents that exceed permissions, resist instructions, or make unauthorized or hidden transactions. In the same week, Rory Madden predicted 80% of his product's users will arrive through an agent, not the UI. Agent experience (AX) is becoming a surface you design, with its own failure modes. [Apple 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000320193/000032019326000026/aapl-20260926.htm) · [The Product Model](https://blog.uxdx.com/newsletters/product-model/the-product-model-317-are-we-designing-interfaces-nobody-will-use/)

**Share with the team**
- John Cutler: AI removed the friction, but some of it was useful. Pairing, revision rounds and "is this worth it?" checks now have to be designed back in on purpose. [The Beautiful Mess](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-450-the-loss-of-positive-friction)
- Elena Verna: almost nobody discovers new features, and AI makes discovery moot. The growth lever becomes trust: reliability, transparency, undo. [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/cp/223665135)
- Claire Vo's agent benchmark: four approval prompts vs one made the best agent feel like a babysitting job. Permission frequency is a design decision. [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-i-ai-grok-vs-dot-vs-muse-vs-duet)

## Thesis check
- **Commerce:** Complicated again. Being agent-mediated is splitting in two: assistants inside the store (Amazon, Shopify's AI referrals) are growing, while open third-party agents are still measured in single digits.
- **UX practice:** Strengthened. Verna, Vo and Apple's 10-K all put the value in trust, permission and failure behavior, and Cutler adds that team process itself now needs designing.
