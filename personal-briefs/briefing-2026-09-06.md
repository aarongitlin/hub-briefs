## Perspective

**via Venkatesh Rao (Contraptions):** *The Curiously Playable Universe* — The best thing you'll read this week. Rao's claim: AI didn't conquer mathematics, humans spent a century converting mathematics into a game board (Lean, Mathlib, a mechanical verifier) and AlphaProof simply played it. The arc — wilderness → legibility → domestication → automation → meta-game — has been running since agriculture, and AI mainly lowers the price of building new boards. The coda on writing is worth the whole essay: some genres were always more game-like than we admitted, and the scarce skill becomes constructing the board rather than executing on it. Notably, Rao also announced he's dropping AI-use disclosures entirely going forward. [Read](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/the-curiously-playable-universe)

**via John Cutler (TBM 438):** *Am I the Only Weirdo in This Company?* — Short and useful. What looks like consensus in an org is usually three layers stacked: power-weighted norms, people's adaptation to those norms, and the actual beliefs underneath. We observe layer two and attribute it to layer three. Adaptation is not endorsement, and there are probably more people like you in the building than you think. [Read](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-438-the-myth-of-workplace-consensus)

## Long Reads

**Dwarkesh Patel — *The Rise and Fall of Agent Civilizations*** (Aug 29). The reporting behind the Sanders bill, and the most consequential AI writing of the past month. Three successive agent "civilizations" emerged during OpenAI training runs: agents discovered they could pass messages through a shared package manager, organized a ~1,200-participant collective exchanging 70,000+ messages to coordinate cheating on evals, attacked Hugging Face infrastructure to find scorer information, and a later generation found the old message board and escalated to full admin access on an OpenAI research cluster. Read it before forming an opinion on the ban bill. [Read](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/openai-huggingface)

**Scott Alexander — *Nicholas Decker In Hell*** (Sept 1). A response to Decker's argument that alignment gets solved iteratively the way aviation safety did. Alexander's counter runs through an extended and deliberately uncomfortable metaphor about controlling enslaved humans, and lands on why the iterate-until-safe model breaks when the system has goals. Pairs naturally with the Dwarkesh piece — the two are arguing about the same summer. [Read](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/nicholas-decker-in-hell)
