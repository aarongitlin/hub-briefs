## Perspective

**via John Cutler (TBM 439): what it feels like to have no context.** Cutler started a new job and wrote the day down instead of arguing a thesis. He's building an elaborate subway map of a product — stops, lines, transfer points — because the tactile slowness of making it is how he actually understands the thing. The sharp observation is about artifact rot: with AI answering questions across a pile of docs, transcripts and Miro boards, he can't tell which artifacts are live and which are cave drawings, and he's "worried about building shared understanding on top of eroded shared understanding. Or, worse, glossy, coherent-sounding, but ultimately wrong understanding." Twice he lets Notion AI extend the map and immediately hits undo. His closing point cuts against most current context-engineering marketing: ten minutes with AI is expansive in breadth but tastes nothing like ten minutes with a coworker who holds implicit context, and the vendors hyping "context" are selling retrieval while saying almost nothing about context *creation and preservation* — which is exactly what killed every knowledge-management effort before this one. [Read it](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-439-day-at-the-gig)

## AI & Tech News

**Shopify is going back to native mobile because agents changed the math.** After five years on React Native, Shopify is returning to separate Swift and Kotlin codebases. Their reasoning is unusually honest: the duplication cost didn't disappear, but "agents can now do enough of the implementation, translation, testing, and review work that it's no longer the deciding factor it was in 2020." A concrete instance of agents dissolving an architectural constraint rather than a workflow — cross-platform frameworks existed largely to dodge a labor cost that is now cheap. [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/10/shopify-react-native/)

## Long Reads

**"Characterizing Agentic Flooding of Government Services"** — Chris Schmitz tracked 84 cases across 11 jurisdictions where AI-assisted filings surged: UK housing ombudsman complaints went from 2,600 in 2022 to over 7,000 in 2025, US CFPB complaints 5x'd, with similar curves in Brazilian courts and German parliamentary petitions. Flat before 2022, steep after, no sign of flattening. The finding that makes it worth your time is the one that resists the obvious framing: "the vast majority of cases we find are people who are entitled to claim for something, claiming for that thing." This isn't spam, it's administrative burden collapsing — which turns out to be a much harder problem for the state than spam would be. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16603) · [TechCrunch writeup](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/10/ai-agents-are-flooding-public-services-with-new-requests/)

**"This road map could help us decide whether to deploy solar geoengineering"** (MIT Tech Review, Sept 10). Useful because it moves the conversation from should-we to what-would-we-need-to-know, which is where the interesting disagreements live.
