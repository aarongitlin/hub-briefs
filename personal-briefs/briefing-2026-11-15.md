## From Your Inbox

**via Lenny's Newsletter: How Meta's Muse became the year's breakout consumer AI product.** Mona Sarantakos, VP of Product at Meta Superintelligence Labs, gives her first podcast interview. Muse is #1 in the App Store. It started as a weekend hack with OpenClaw on a Raspberry Pi, then waited seven months to launch even though it started before most rivals. Topics include how Nat Friedman, Alex Wang and Zuckerberg each shaped it, the "agent swarms" the team uses to find user insights, and the two levers she says actually move activation for an agent. The email carries only the episode outline, so this is a pointer, not a summary. Pair it with the companion How I AI episode with designer Alex Cornell on how Muse was designed. **via Design Better: Build AI toys to beat AI-tool anxiety.** Eli Woolery says the top worry in their 2026 State of Design survey is keeping up with the flood of AI tools. His fix is to build something. He made a pixel-art robot on a $50 Ulanzi LED display that waves when Claude Code needs him. The Notification hook never fired, so Claude designed a workaround: start a timer on each tool call, and wave if it's still running after a few seconds. He also built a Tesla solar dashboard that texts him via ntfy.sh when to plug in the car, and polls every 10 minutes to stay inside Tesla's API free tier. Both run 24/7 on a Raspberry Pi, and both repos are public. It's close to what your Mac mini hub is doing. [Design Better](https://designbetterpodcast.com/p/the-roundup-the-best-way-to-learn) · [claude-pixel-bot](https://github.com/ewoolery/claude-pixel-bot) · [solar-energy-dashboard](https://github.com/ewoolery/solar-energy-dashboard)

**via Superhuman AI (Sunday science special): Starship gets caught by the tower after a full orbital flight.** After a 22-hour orbital trip, the Starship upper stage flew back to Texas and was caught by the launch tower's arms, a first for the ship. The booster wasn't caught. It's a big step toward full reuse. Also in the issue: researchers used AI to predict how a blind man's visual cortex would respond to a 96-electrode implant, so they could choose stimulation patterns that produced the shapes they intended. It hasn't restored sight yet. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/spacexs-starship-aces-first-return-texas-2026-11-14/) · [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261110042553.htm)

**via Rands in Repose: "The Old Guard," revisited.** Michael Lopp reflects on his "Second Act" talk, which he gave almost unchanged to company after company. Its thesis comes from Palantir and Pinterest: the people who got you to your first success can become the obstacle to the next one. His takeaway is to read your writing out loud, because "the truth just sounds different." It's a short reflection, not new material. It's timely given how many AI companies are going through their own second act. [Rands](https://randsinrepose.com/archives/the-old-guard/)
