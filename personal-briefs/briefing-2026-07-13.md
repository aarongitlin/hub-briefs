## From Your Inbox

**via Ben Evans: Model week — the leapfrog pattern holds.** OpenAI's new 5.6 models caught back up to Anthropic's Fable after a couple of weeks of Anthropic leading; meanwhile Grok's latest release put xAI back near the pareto frontier on price/performance and Meta's rebuilt lab shipped Muse Spark, with Zuckerberg now offering Meta's models in the cloud to developers for the first time. Ben's take: no one has a sustainable lead, and it's still unclear what the structural barriers to entry are beyond cash.

**via Ben Evans: ChatGPT Work launches, Atlas dies, Simo departs.** OpenAI launched ChatGPT Work (its answer to Anthropic's Cowork — Ben calls the app experience "incredibly chaotic, confused and messy"), discontinued its Atlas browser, and lost product/business chief Fidji Simo, who stepped down entirely after a medical leave. Another management restructure follows.

**via Ben Evans: Apple sues OpenAI over trade secrets.** Apple filed suit against OpenAI, hardware chief Tang Tan, and Chang Liu, alleging taken proprietary data, continued post-departure system access, and coaching of departing Apple employees (400+ former Apple engineers now work at OpenAI). Bad timing for OpenAI's hardware ambitions — and its IPO.

**via Ben Evans: AI export wars go both directions.** Reuters reports Beijing is weighing export controls on Chinese open-source models (DeepSeek + Qwen are ~25% of OpenRouter usage amid the token pricing crunch), while the FT caught OpenAI and Google selling model access to Singapore subsidiaries of blacklisted Alibaba, Baidu, and Tencent.

**via Ben Evans: "Most people aren't tool-builders."** This week's column argues vibe-coding runs into the same three walls that killed the bottom-up SaaS dream: most people don't see the problem, aren't tool-builders even when they do, and aren't positioned to deploy the tool anyway. Writing the code was never the hard part. Pairs with his new essay on token pricing economics: [ben-evans.com](https://www.ben-evans.com/)

## Perspective

**via John Cutler: TBM 430 — Incubate, Compound, Refinance, Liquidate.** Treats your software portfolio like a financial one, plotted on value created vs. carrying cost. The insight isn't where an asset sits but which direction it's moving and whether that movement is intentional — and AI's critical question becomes "faster in which direction?", since AI can accelerate exactly the wrong motion. Recasts tech debt as a financing choice. [Read](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-430-incubate-compound-refinance)

**via Venkatesh Rao: The Taste Essay.** A theory of taste cultures built from Mrs. Higgins in *Pygmalion*: the fashionable, the unfashionable, the indifferent, the pioneer, and the transcendent — with kindness as "the ultimate act of tastelessness." The AI kicker: today we can only teach models connoisseurship (a "Large Taste Model" — learned automaticity, distillable into weights); real taste requires exposure to aesthetic and social risk, which models don't yet bear. [Read](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/the-taste-essay)
