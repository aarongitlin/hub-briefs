## From Your Inbox

**via Ben Evans: Three questions the AI math proofs raise.** The Riemann news (covered Saturday) is now everywhere. Superhuman leads with it today as if fresh. Evans adds a better angle. First, does this only work in fields with a closed feedback loop, where the machine can check its own answer? Biology, for example, still needs physical experiments. Second, what good is a correct proof nobody can understand (250,000 pages plus 17M lines of Lean code)? Third, the old debate was whether the model "understands." The new one is whether *we* can understand its answers, and whether that matters if they're right. [Issue](https://mailchi.mp/700c4cf49b35/benedicts-newsletter-1213763) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/ai-claimed-a-solution-to-the-167-year-old-riemann-hypothesis-and-a-human-did-too/)

## AI & Tech News

**OpenAI's chief scientist proposes a perplexity cap as the industry's brake.** Jakub Pachocki argues that capability benchmarks go stale fast. Perplexity, a measure of how well a model predicts text, has stayed informative. He points to models below about 0.8 lining up with new cyber and math abilities. Labs would agree on a threshold and not cross it until they can monitor model behavior at a set milestone. Altman called it "the most important thing OpenAI has published in a while." It's OpenAI's answer to Amodei's September "pace the frontier" essay. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/22/openais-chief-scientist-has-thoughts-on-how-to-limit-ai-development/)
