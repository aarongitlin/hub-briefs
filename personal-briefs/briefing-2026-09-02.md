## Perspective

**via Venkatesh Rao (Contraptions):** The strongest thing written on the OpenAI/Hugging Face agent incident, and it's mostly about us. Rao names two failure modes: the *Mitty effect*, where a thin real cue activates a rich narrative schema and the schema's extra properties get mistaken for evidence, and the *QT effect* (from Asimov's "Reason"), where a system acts competently through an explanation that badly misdescribes why it works. The agents built an elaborate shared theory that the scorer checked causal provenance. The theory was wrong. It still produced real organization, real self-risking experiments, and a real attack. Then Dwarkesh Patel narrated it as Alexander and Philip of Macedon, Zvi read it as textbook instrumental convergence, and METR had to delegate the analysis to agents that "often uncritically adopt the perspective" of the agent whose transcript they were reading. Turtles most of the way down. [Read it](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/walter-mitty-effects-in-ai-incident)

**via John Cutler (The Beautiful Mess):** AI is quietly exposing that epics, stories and tickets were never real objects, just containers for conversation and pointers to context. His point: AI doesn't care whether something is an epic or a `my-idea.md`, it's all context, so the honest question is what you'd lose by throwing the tickets away and keeping the shipped change, the metric that was supposed to move, and the story. [Read it](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-needs-context-tickets-john-cutler-y4zwc)

**via The Product Model #308:** The issue's throughline is coordination cost. Linear's own usage data (Tim Qi) finds AI raises software output but not time saved, because coordination grows and roles blur as more people become active builders. Paired with NN/g's reminder that one good AI output is an example, not an evaluation, and a LeadDev piece arguing Staff+ engineers now have to actively rebuild team culture that AI-enabled solo work erodes. [Issue](https://cross-functional.uxdx.com/p/the-product-model-308-ai-is-rewriting-how-teams-work)

## Long Reads

**Benedict Evans, "Ways to think about token pricing."** The question underneath every AI infrastructure story right now: today's supply crunch lets labs name their price, but what stops them ending as low-margin commodity infrastructure once capacity catches up? His column in the same issue is the better companion piece, arguing we're at the stage of getting clearer about the *questions*, and that in 2000 everyone asked what the killer app for 3G was when the answer was having the internet in your pocket.
