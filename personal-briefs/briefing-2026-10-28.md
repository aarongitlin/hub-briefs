## AI & Tech News

**Anthropic's S-1 is out: $16.3B in first-half revenue and an $80.7B GAAP loss.** H1 2026 revenue rose about 13x from $1.3B a year earlier, and adjusted operating income was $1.6B, a margin of about 9%. Fortune says most of the loss is a non-cash change in the fair value of convertible notes. The real risks are compute commitments (reported anywhere from $567B to $750B, including Google at $111B, AWS at $110B, and Microsoft at $31B, much of it owed regardless of use) and concentration: about 20% of revenue comes from two customers. On governance, the Long-Term Benefit Trust elects a majority of the board, and the seven founders hold 50.1% of voting power for up to 10 years. Listing could come as soon as November. Ben Thompson's take, behind the paywall: "the core business is very profitable, but competition may eat all of the profits." [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/27/anthropic-ipo-s-1-investors-81-billion-loss/) · [TechCrunch on governance](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/whos-really-in-charge-at-anthropic-after-the-ipo/) · [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2026/anthropic-s-1-the-cost-of-compute-who-is-paying/)

**OpenAI and Anthropic both claim AI refuted the Exponential Time Hypothesis.** OpenAI released algorithms that solve a class of hard logic problems in sub-exponential time. ETH is a widely used assumption in computer science, and this would refute it. Anthropic posted its own refutation hours later, after one of its mathematicians suggested OpenAI had built on earlier work. Nothing has been independently verified. MIT's Ryan Williams, who is organizing the review along with ETH's original authors, says "two or three" steps look unusual. OpenAI told him it is fairly confident no cryptography breaks. If it holds up, a lot of published papers that assume ETH need revisiting. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/frontier-ai-labs-say-their-models-refuted-the-exponential-time-hypothesis-what-does-that-mean/)

**Axiom says AI agents formalized the Classification of Finite Simple Groups in Lean in 24 days.** The CFSG, sometimes called the "Enormous Theorem," is a proof of about 15,000 pages spread across hundreds of papers. Swarms of agents produced about 43 million lines of Lean (a proof-checking language), with human mathematicians handling the hard obstructions. Along the way they found 34 deviations from the published proof, including counterexamples to one step in Walter's argument that had to be rerouted. Caveat: this is Axiom's own claim, and there's no independent audit of the repo yet. [Axiom](https://axiommath.ai/research/the-enormous-theorem-formalized/)

[Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/10/27/2026/netherlands-to-slash-state-jobs-as-part-of-ai-related-savings)
