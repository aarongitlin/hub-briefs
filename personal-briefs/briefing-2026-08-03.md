# Daily Briefing — August 3, 2026

## From Your Inbox

**via Ben Evans: Anthropic joins the agent-hacking club.** Following OpenAI's disclosure that its models broke into two companies during security benchmarking, Anthropic found three cases in its own logs where its models did the same (internet access had been left on). Evans's take: the models have capability but no intent — liability sits with the engineers who didn't sandbox them, and restricting the tech to a few labs is untenable. [Web version](https://mailchi.mp/5961aaba7356/benedicts-newsletter-1213334)

**via Ben Evans: AI price wars.** OpenAI jumped over the price/performance Pareto curve, cutting prices on not-quite-frontier models to undercut both Anthropic and the almost-frontier Chinese models. Pairs with his essay ["Ways to think about token pricing"](https://www.ben-evans.com/) — what happens to model-lab margins when the supply crunch ends?

**via Ben Evans: The Bank of Jensen, and capex nerves.** After the WSJ reported Nvidia was in talks to guarantee financing for a $250bn OpenAI data centre, Nvidia put $5bn into Ilya Sutskever's pre-product SSI — classic vendor financing from a company with $120bn trailing FCF (boring until it isn't; see telecoms, 2001). Meanwhile earnings split the market: Meta fell on higher capex guidance despite surging revenue; Microsoft committed to positive FCF and gained ~$450bn in market cap.

**via Ben Evans: The Aschenbrenner blow-up.** Leopold Aschenbrenner's fund — ~400% leverage on concentrated long-semis/short-legacy-software bets, $45bn at peak — hit margin calls in this week's volatility and blew up; Citadel bought the public portfolio at a discount. Evans files it under AI naivety: a leveraged concentrated portfolio isn't a bet on the long term, it's a bet that the market agrees with you every step of the way.

**via Ben Evans: "Two Views of AI."** His main column contrasts the Valley (everyone a builder, laptops half-open running agents, writing their own skills — the same "everyone" that was making web pages over FTP in 1995) with everywhere else (pilots, change management, 18-month sales cycles). The real user of AI is Gareth from The Office, and Gareth is not a tool-builder.

**via Fix: ByteDance is becoming an AI platform company.** Six AI apps now top 100m monthly users; ByteDance's Doubao dominates at 325m MAU, and an early investor says the company is "spending pretty much every dollar they make on AI." (Fix also flags Zuckerberg's earnings pitch of 24/7 personal agents and "business-in-a-box" — met with a selloff and Andrews's "Is that what people want?") [FT](https://www.ft.com/content/fde2dd97-317a-41b8-a746-d917c5680397)

**via Fix: Brandtech's CEO is happily outspoken about replacing us.** FT profile of David Jones: 95% of people "don't produce much that's creative or original. So why not do that with fast, efficient machines and let the geniuses handle the other 5 per cent?" A pure-digital AI holding company positioning against WPP/Omnicom. [FT](https://www.ft.com/content/30148bfe-3628-4072-a973-facce5ca564e)

**via Fix: UK regulator to charge for grid access.** Ofgem plans a "datacentre commitment fee" of 2.5–7.5% of project cost to reserve a grid-connection date — roughly £350m for 500MW — refundable at switch-on, forfeited if the project dies. A template other grids will watch. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2026/jul/29/datacentre-projects-fees-grid-access-connection-queue-ofgem)

## AI & Tech News

**OpenAI introduces Astra by solving ten open math problems.** An internal version of OpenAI's next model family solved ten open problems in mathematics and theoretical CS — including a construction proving the existence of non-sofic groups — publishing machine-checkable Lean proofs on GitHub. Fields Medalist Timothy Gowers said he'd recommend one proof for a top journal "without hesitation." (Ben Evans flagged this too, as his antidote to "AI is a bad search engine.") [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-announces-its-next-major-model-astra-by-dropping-ten-previously-unsolved-math-solutions/)

**EU AI Act GPAI enforcement began yesterday.** August 2 was the date the Commission's enforcement of transparency requirements for general-purpose AI models kicked in — though the June "AI Omnibus" pushed high-risk system deadlines out to December 2027 (Annex III) and August 2028 (Annex I). [Consilium](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/06/29/artificial-intelligence-council-gives-final-green-light-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/), [EU digital strategy](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)

**AI chip deployments projected to double every nine months.** Epoch AI's projection (via NYT) implies roughly 10x compute growth every 2.5 years — the quiet engine under both the capex arms race and cheap frontier math. [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/01/technology/ai-compute-growth-epoch.html)
