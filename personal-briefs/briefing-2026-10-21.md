## AI & Tech News

**Anthropic drafts an AI safety bill with a hard stop.** In a draft shared with lawmakers, regulators could block a model's release if it poses a "foreseeable, imminent risk" of 50+ deaths or $1B+ in damage from bioweapons, a cyberattack, or a model evading its developer's control. Evaluators registered with the Commerce Department would test models, paid by the labs but not based on results. On a finding, they'd have 72 hours to notify Commerce, which would ask a D.C. judge to halt release. It goes well past rivals' positions, like Nvidia's view that no new AI laws are needed. Whether lab-paid evaluators can stay independent is the open question. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/20/anthropic-pitches-ai-safety-bill-that-may-block-a-model-if-50-people-could-be-killed/)

**Managers say AI made their jobs harder, for now.** A Nielsen survey of 1,700 directors and senior managers, commissioned by Monday.com, finds over half say it's harder to evaluate employees' work since AI arrived, because individual contribution is harder to see. Nearly half spend more time reviewing work, and 39% use AI to draft performance reviews. 58% expect to oversee more AI agents than people within a year. Consider the sponsor: Monday.com sells the fix. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/21/ai-promises-productivity-but-for-managers-its-mostly-a-headache-for-now-says-new-survey/)

## Long Reads

**Scott Alexander, "Against Against Handover."** He separates "successionism," where AIs replace humans, from "handoverism," where AIs run institutions on humans' behalf because they'd do it better. He walks through five escalating versions, from AI soldiers aligned to democracy and a hypothetical "President Claude" to personal guardian-angel AIs that vote for you and a night-watchman superintelligence. He argues that rejecting all of them means accepting human rulers' track record. It's provocative and worth arguing with. [Astral Codex Ten](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/against-against-handover)

**Ben Thompson, "Do You Trust Apple?"** Apple's HomeView and Home Collection are a good smart-home pitch: tight integration, privacy, and no separate app or account. But the whole thing rides on voice, which means betting on a Siri AI that's slower and makes more mistakes than the old one. Thompson ties it to Apple's dependence on partners' models, which cuts against the old Cook Doctrine of owning the core tech. [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2026/do-you-trust-apple/)
