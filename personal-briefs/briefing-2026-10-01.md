## Perspective

**via John Cutler (TBM 442):** **10 Career Traps Thoughtful People Fall Into.** Each trap starts as a strength — curiosity, empathy, pattern recognition, a willingness to question — and closes slowly. The Volunteer Trap: you adopt an orphaned problem and become illegible, so at layoff time nobody can explain what you do. The Right But Early Trap: right is not right now. The Canary Trap: the more you notice incoherence, the more of your attention it takes, until tracking the dysfunction replaces the job. Cutler's actual contribution is the idea of a **saveable moment** — a specific, rehearsable tripwire per trap, like "you asked the question twice and people started giving you feedback on how you asked it rather than answering it." Useful whether or not you buy the taxonomy. [Read it](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-442-10-career-traps-thoughtful)

## Long Reads

**The Ugly Economics of Consumer AI** — Russell Brandom, TechCrunch, September 30. The sharpest number you will read this week: as of May 2026 only 2.2% of consumers paid for AI, averaging $31/month. Even at Netflix scale — 325 million subscribers — that is $11B a year, less than a third of OpenAI's operating costs. Which is why dots, Muse, and Instinct are launches, not strategies, and why OpenAI's enterprise bookings doubling since July is the real story. [Read it](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/30/the-ugly-economics-of-consumer-ai/)

**Apps, Agents, and Aggregation** — Ben Thompson, Stratechery, September 28. Thompson's argument: the web made distribution abundant, which made *discovery* scarce, which is what Google and Meta aggregated. Agents make execution abundant, so the newly scarce thing is volition — knowing what you actually want done. Whoever solves inspiration gains power over everyone with things that need doing, and apps become implementation details. The best frame going for what the DevDay launches are really competing over. [Read it](https://stratechery.com/2026/apps-agents-and-aggregation/)
