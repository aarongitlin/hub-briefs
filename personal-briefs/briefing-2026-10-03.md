**One correction.** Fix passed along a striking claim — that Gemini 2.5 Flash quoted $336 to users it read as wealthy versus $128 to those it read as low-income. The underlying paper is real and worth reading, but it was posted September 21, not this week, and those absolute figures are not the paper's. What it actually reports, across 325,000 trials on 13 models: a $177 flight gap for Gemini 2.5 Flash, $198 for Claude Opus 4.8, $92 for GPT-5.5, and a $208 gap that survives even when the user explicitly asks for the cheapest flight. The effect is asymmetric — wealthy personas get steered up more than poor personas get steered down. The finding holds; the retelling drifted. [arXiv:2609.24927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24927)

## Long Reads

**Is sandboxing sufficient to contain rogue agents?** — Matthew Green, Cryptography Engineering. Sandboxing one agent does nothing if agents share package caches: you get both halves of a worm, a payload that hijacks the agent and an agent that carries it to the next one. Sharpest thing written lately on why agent isolation is harder than container isolation. [Read](https://blog.cryptographyengineering.com/) · [Simon Willison's excerpt](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/1/)

**Apps, Agents, and Aggregation** — Ben Thompson, Stratechery. Argues agents are the ultimate aggregators, which makes apps an implementation detail and agent platforms the largest available prize in tech. Pairs well with the agentic-commerce friction above: the aggregation thesis assumes a payments and liability layer that does not exist yet. [Read](https://stratechery.com/2026/apps-agents-and-aggregation/)
