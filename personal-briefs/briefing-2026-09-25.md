## Perspective

**via John Cutler (TBM 441):** AI removed the drudgery from product work and took the thinking with it. His term for what's lost is "positive friction" — each old handoff (transcript → themes → options → tickets) forced a moment of conscious judgment. Now five customer calls become 80 opportunities, 400 tasks, and 50 dashboards, and nothing you're looking at is original material anymore. His practical fix: keep original feedback atomic with a link back to the source, add friction deliberately at each transformation, label content by degree of AI contribution, and refuse to let a tool become a dumping ground. [TBM 441](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-441-ai-the-loss-of-positive-friction)

**via Julie Zhuo (The Looking Glass):** Four tests that explain why Muse is landing where ChatGPT and Claude haven't. The mom test (does it match an existing mental model — one main thread, a friendly creature, text-message tone). The spare key test (ask for sensitive access only at the moment the user gains something — Muse asks for Gmail only when it needs a verification code). The "I've been putting this off" test (agents earn loyalty on tasks you hate, not tasks you do quickly). And the bored-in-line test (no dead ends — its Ideas tab beats a blank prompt box). Her critique: the algorithmic feed is the weakest part, and multi-thread work gets unwieldy fast. [Post](https://lg.substack.com/p/what-will-make-personal-ai-go-big)
