## From Your Inbox

**via Superhuman AI: Bigger models and max reasoning are often the wrong default.** New research says higher reasoning effort often fails on tasks that lower settings solve, and costs up to 42x more on simple work. Superhuman's examples: Fermisense spent $500 to train a 9B open model that beat five frontier models while running 68x cheaper. Nvidia and CrowdStrike built a security system at 99% lower cost. The practical advice is to build a small internal benchmark from your own tasks and switch models only when one wins on it. Revolut is a real-world case: The Register reports it cut AI coding costs about 90% by making self-hosted GLM models its default and calling commercial models only when needed. ## Perspective

**via Venkatesh Rao (Substack note): AI is a "DIY-market-making technology."** Rao argues AI isn't making familiar consumer goods cheaper. Companies use it to make their *inputs* cheaper, not their products. The consumer surplus shows up one step upstream, as substitution: "AI often doesn't make X cheaper but it allows you to replace it with Y which was already cheaper." That makes the gains look smaller in standard price data than they are. [Note](https://substack.com/@contraptions/note/c-374709654)

**via John Cutler (Substack note): When building stops being scarce.** Cutler asks what happens to people whose identity and influence came from being the ones who could build. Their expertise used to decide what got built, how long it took, and which trade-offs were made. Now that software is cheap to produce, that scarcity, and the power that came with it, is fading. It's a short note, but a good question for any design or engineering leader. [Note](https://substack.com/@cutlefish/note/c-375410009)

**Anthropic publishes FlashProve, a way to prove which model produced a response.** It uses cryptographic proofs to verify that an output came from the claimed model without revealing the weights. This matters for anyone buying "Claude via a third party" or auditing agents. It's experimental research with code, and no production plans have been announced. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/provable-inference) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/anthropic-is-working-on-a-system-to-prove-ai-models-identity/)
