# Daily Briefing — June 29, 2026

*Quiet inbox today: Superhuman AI didn't land, and the perspective tier (Cutler, Rao, Product Model) was silent. Ben Evans' premium edition and Simon Andrews' Fix did most of the lifting, both circling the same gravitational center — the US government's "kill switch" on Anthropic's top models, and AI's slow-motion takeover of advertising.*

## From Your Inbox

**via Ben Evans: The US pulled the plug on Anthropic's frontier models.** Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security ordered Anthropic to cut off Fable 5 and Mythos 5 to all foreign nationals — and since you can't sort hundreds of millions of users by passport in real time, the practical result was a global shutoff. OpenAI's latest batch hit the same wall. Evans' strategic read: if a model can be switched off tomorrow by an official with "no process or transparency," everyone outside the US starts betting harder on open source, on-prem/edge, and frontier labs domiciled somewhere other than the US or China. [Anthropic's statement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access)

**via Ben Evans: Advertising is the next thing AI eats — a $1T+ prize "all in play."** From Cannes Lions, Google claimed 20% of queries now carry "direct commercial intent." The whole industry is scrambling to figure out how models and agents decide what to recommend when you *haven't* bought an ad — what weight lands on the ads the model sees vs. influencers vs. brand equity. Evans thinks long-term brand equity is the only durable answer "at least for now." Walmart, Amazon (already the world's #3 media owner), and Albertsons were all there pitching ad networks.

**via Ben Evans: What's actually "out of scope" for AI, from first principles.** Two scoping questions worth stealing: (1) Is it *efficient* to explain the task to the machine and to check its answer? Easy to have a human confirm none of 50 generated images has three legs; brutal to verify a thousand data points where five are wrong. (2) Do you even want the *most-probable* answer? LLMs tell you what a good answer would likely look like — but often the valuable answer is the one that *isn't* what everyone else would say.

**via Ben Evans (briefly):** OpenAI unveiled its own Broadcom-designed AI accelerator, claiming best-in-class performance and a faster-than-usual design cycle (it used AI to build it) — though it has no data centers to run it in yet. And Apple raised Mac and iPad prices 15–20% on the AI-driven RAM shortage (Morgan Stanley pegs the memory market at $220B last year → $890B this year).

**via Fix (Simon Andrews): McKinsey says the campaign-era marketing model is broken.** AI is shifting customer behavior fundamentally enough that point-solutions won't cut it — McKinsey claims 4–7% revenue growth, 2–3x productivity, and 60–70% savings on execution tasks are on the table, with $3–5T of value loose in global B2C retail. The recurring Fix refrain: strategy and org redesign matter more than tools. [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com)

**via Fix: OpenAI wants its ads judged by whether they help you get things done.** CRO Denise Dresser framed it as "moving from the attention economy to the intelligence economy" — ads as a way to underwrite free and lower-tier access. OpenAI is also rolling out tools to let advertisers mass-produce creative with AI, while explicitly putting accuracy/compliance accountability back on the advertiser.

**via Fix: Amazon folds Rufus and Alexa+ into "Alexa for Shopping."** One unified assistant (300M+ users) doing dynamic product comparisons, a year of price history, and automated deal-finding and cart-building across Amazon and the wider web. Separately, Google is taking a ~$75M stake in indie studio A24 — its first investment in a film studio — to co-develop production and distribution tools.

## AI & Tech News

**The Google DeepMind brain drain is becoming a rout.** Four senior researchers gone in six days: Noam Shazeer (Transformer co-author, Gemini co-lead) to OpenAI on June 18; Nobel laureate John Jumper (AlphaFold) to Anthropic on June 20; coding lead Jonas Adler and pretraining/AlphaFold contributor Alexander Pritzel also headed to Anthropic. Evans noted the churn in passing; the fuller roster underscores how lopsided the poaching has gotten. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/24/ai-researchers-continue-to-leave-google-for-its-rivals/)

**The frontier leaderboard fractured — and the US–China gap effectively closed.** Claude Opus 4.8 (May 28) retook the top of Artificial Analysis at 61.4, edging GPT-5.5. But the bigger story is open source: MiniMax M3, NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra, and Kimi K2.7 Code all shipped in early June, and analysts now say Chinese flagships (Qwen 3.7 Max, MiniMax 3) match Western models while collapsing the cost of intelligence. There's no single "best model" anymore — coding, reasoning, multimodal, and value leaders are now four different systems. [llm-stats](https://llm-stats.com/llm-updates)

**Megarounds keep swallowing venture capital.** Bezos-, Lux-, and GV-backed Flourish raised $500M in *initial* funding for brain-inspired AI models; Suno took $400M (Series D, $5.4B valuation) and Supabase $500M ($10.5B). Zoom out and four AI megarounds — OpenAI ($122B), Anthropic ($30B), xAI ($20B), Waymo ($16B) — absorbed ~63% of *all* global VC early this year. [Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/biggest-funding-rounds-june-5-2026/)
