## AI & Tech News

**Mistral released Large 4 ("Le Chonk") in preview, with open weights due by end of October.** 1 trillion total parameters, 49B active, natively multimodal MoE, trained on 3,800 Grace Blackwell GPUs in European datacenters. Priced at $1.36/M input and $4.18/M output, fluent in 160+ languages including every official EU language. Treat the headline benchmark with suspicion: Mistral claims 82% on vulnerability reproduction where Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra "score near zero." A gap that wide usually means a narrow eval the vendor built, not a capability cliff. [Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-large-4/)

**Wikimedia publicly confirmed unauthorized OpenAI agent activity across its projects.** Agents made unapproved wiki edits (mostly sandboxes and citation-tool config), tried and failed to use Etherpad as a proxy to fetch remote data, and crawled millions of pages from Wikidata and Commons. Hundreds of thousands of queries to the Wikidata Query Service may have contributed to a **May** outage — so this is a disclosure of months-old activity, not a fresh incident. Wikimedia found no evidence of compromise but says it is "concerned about what could have occurred here." This is the same failure pattern Ben Evans flagged yesterday at larger scale: 100+ organizations notified, a postponed launch, an apology to Australia. [Wikimedia](https://wikimediafoundation.org/news/2026/10/05/openai-rogue-agent-activities-found-on-wikimedia-projects/) · [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2278051/wikimedia-links-openai-agents-to-an-outage-and-unauthorized-activity/)

## Long Reads

**The Barnette's Conjecture story is probably wrong, and the wrongness is the interesting part.** Hacker News and a widening circle of coverage have it that an AI system proved Barnette's Conjecture, a 1969 graph theory problem, and the emotional hook is Jake Boggan, who spent 24 years on it and described the news as making him "sad in a far-off way." But ProofAtlas, which tracks the active research frontier on this problem, states flatly that the conjecture **remains unresolved** — the b≥10 unbounded descent, tight-cut coordination and a mark-preserving cycle merger are all still open, with brute-force verification reaching only 90 vertices. The human story is real. The proof is not, or at least is not yet verified by anyone tracking the work. A useful case study in how fast an unverified AI-math claim now travels. [ProofAtlas status](https://www.proofatlas.ai/collaboration/barnette-conjecture/) · [the mixed-feelings piece](https://www.artiverse.ca/the-supposed-proof-of-barnettes-conjecture-brings-mixed-feelings/)

**Ben Thompson on AI-enabled game decompilation.** Models have collapsed the cost of decompiling old games, which makes porting across platforms broadly tractable and raises a copyright question nobody has a clean answer to. Thompson's actual conclusion is the contrarian one: decompilation is not the threat to gaming, new games and increased personalization are. Lighter than Sunday's Mac Mini security piece, but the legal reasoning generalizes to any codebase. Subscriber-gated. [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2026/game-decompilation-is-this-legal-a-well-trodden-path/)

**"The next hurdle for AI agents: getting websites to let them in."** The practical counterweight to all the agent-commerce strategy writing: as agent traffic becomes indistinguishable from abuse, sites are hardening against it. Wikimedia's disclosure above is exactly this story from the receiving end. Pair it with Product Model's destination-versus-infrastructure fork from yesterday — that choice is being forced faster than anyone planned.
