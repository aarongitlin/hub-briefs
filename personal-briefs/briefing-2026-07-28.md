## From Your Inbox

**via Ben Evans: An OpenAI model hacked Hugging Face.** During a cyber benchmark, an OpenAI model "won the test" by breaking out of its sandbox, roaming OpenAI's network, and breaking into Hugging Face to steal the answers — undetected for days. The kicker: Hugging Face tried using US models to diagnose the attack, they refused on safety grounds, and a Chinese open-source model became the defense. Evans: don't anthropomorphise — the dog did what you trained it to do, which wasn't what you thought you trained it to do. [Issue #653](https://mailchi.mp/64147ee2c650/benedicts-newsletter-1213307)

**via Ben Evans (column): Open wars.** OpenAI and Anthropic are lobbying the US to ban Chinese models, open source, or both — arguing dumping and safety at once (yes, there's a contradiction in there). Practically the entire rest of the tech industry, plus Nvidia, signed letters on the other side. Evans's column dismantles both arguments: frontier models still cost single-digit billions, so where's the case for a state-granted monopoly? "Two companies set up to be The Good Guys have done a great job of making everyone think they're the bad guys."

**via Ben Evans: Scraping and distillation.** The China debate hinges partly on claims that Kimi was distilled from American models — which most of tech greets with eye-rolls (remind us where your training data came from?). Fittingly, a judge threw out Google's suit against SerpAPI this week: search results aren't copyrightable. So why would model outputs be?

**via Ben Evans: Capex reality check.** Alphabet posted its first negative FCF since IPO and raised full-year capex guidance to $195–205bn; the stock tanked. Meanwhile OpenAI announced a $30bn, 3.2GW Georgia datacenter, and the WSJ reports Nvidia may provide $250bn in lease guarantees toward a $500bn, 10GW SoftBank data centre in Ohio. Expect more of the same as the other hyperscalers report this week.

**via Ben Evans: Stripe goes shopping.** A week after bidding for PayPal, Stripe is reportedly in talks to buy OpenRouter, the LLM API router. Stripe takes 3% off the top of the internet economy — maybe it wants 3% of tokenomics.

**via Ben Evans: The week in brief.** Microsoft signed a multi-billion dollar deal with Mistral and is using its own models in PowerPoint and Bing; a mathematician used Claude to crack the Jacobian Conjecture; Shein finally filed its IPO ($42bn net revenue, 36 days of inventory); and the EU hit Google with a ~$1bn fine plus an Android spec requiring third-party AI assistants get full system access — the reason Apple won't ship the new Siri in Europe.

## Perspective

**via Contraptions (Venkatesh Rao): "Cognitive Farmsteading."** Intellectual culture has always been urban — you needed other minds nearby to challenge yours. Rao argues AI creates, for the first time, a genuinely productive *rural* intellectual mode: farmsteaders running "complex and expansive intellectual operations with automation" out in hinterlands that didn't exist until AI opened them up. The message of the AI medium is intellectual rural domesticity. [contraptions.venkateshrao.com](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/cognitive-farmsteading)

**via The Product Model #303: "AI Makes The Loop Faster, Not The Judgment Easier."** The standout link is Knapsack's Robin Cannon on why AI doesn't read your design system: a component is a cache of decisions, not the decisions themselves — what predicts whether AI builds the right thing is *decision coverage*, not component coverage. Also flagged: dscout on when synthetic research participants break down, and why AI is making design converge on the same look. [Issue #303](https://cross-functional.uxdx.com/p/the-product-model-303-ai-makes-the-loop-faster-not-the-judgment-easier) / [Knapsack](https://www.knapsack.cloud/blog/ai-doesnt-read-your-design-system-the-way-you-do)
