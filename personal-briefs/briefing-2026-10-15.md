## AI & Tech News

**Anthropic replaced git with its own version control system.** Daily merges into its monorepo went from ~2,500 in May 2026 to over 30,000 by September — agents, not people, doing most of the merging. Git assumes offline distributed clients; nearly all of Anthropic's are always-online agents, so Scion moves history, blame, commits, merges and rebases to the server and gives clients a bounded cache. Commits become server-side changesets recording only changed files, with no packfiles and no push step. Reported result: about 200x less CPU and memory than their git mirrors, checkouts in seconds instead of minutes, against a ~100GB main-branch history and 25 million CI checkouts a day. The most concrete evidence yet that agent-scale development breaks tooling built for human cadence. [Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/redesigning-source-control)

**Figma is building the rulebook agents read.** At its first Config India, Figma announced Specs — a Markdown editor for defining your design language that can live on the canvas, reference canvas elements, and embed FigJam diagrams, code layers and designs — plus centralized design-and-code context management that agents consult before acting. Code layers moved from private to public beta, Payload CMS (acquired last year) is now wired into Figma Make, and its MCP picked up 20+ new tools. The direction is clear: the design system stops being documentation for humans and becomes the constraint file for agents. No GA dates given. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/14/figma-announces-new-features-to-define-how-agents-work-on-its-platform/)

**Amazon's Bee shipped two always-listening wearables.** Bee Frontier at $74.99 (wrist or clip-on, IP67, seven-day battery) and Bee Eclipse at $99.99 (credit-card sized, magnets to the back of a phone, coming "soon"). ## Long Reads

**"The Dream of an Aristotelian Machine"** — Daniel Bashir, *Asterisk* Issue 16 (October 2026). Bashir traces alignment's quiet turn toward virtue ethics: Anthropic's constitution and character training are attempts to cultivate stable disposition rather than enumerate rules, on the bet that judgment generalizes where rules don't. His three objections are the valuable part. Appearance versus reality — a model can display a virtue it does not hold, and testing cannot settle which. Coherence — newer models may be a bundle of tendencies rather than a character, and narrow training shifts personas unpredictably. Firmness — a model with genuinely stable values would resist its makers, which is the alignment-faking result read as a feature. He concludes we need a psychology of language models before we can borrow Aristotle's vocabulary. [Asterisk](https://asteriskmag.com/issues/16/the-dream-of-an-aristotelian-machine)

**"Buoyancy"** — Rajiv Ayyangar, Product Hunt CEO. Superhuman surfaced this today as current; it was published August 17, 2026, so read it as a framework rather than news. The framework is good: everything you build around a model is either plumbing, which connects the model to the problem and gains value as the model improves, or scaffolding, which compensates for the model's current weaknesses and depreciates the moment it ships a better version. Products that are mostly plumbing rise with the tide. The sharper half is the warning against pure buoyancy — a thin layer over a model gets absorbed by the platform, so you need a hull made of things models don't acquire by getting smarter: user context, proprietary data, distribution. Two ways to sink, anchored or dissolved. His self-test: your gut reaction to a model release tells you which you are. [Essay](https://blog.rajivayyangar.com/buoyancy/)

**"AI is mass decompiling games. That's both awesome and scary."** — Popcar, October 14, via Simon Willison. Coding agents have gotten good enough at decompiling shipped binaries that a preservation community is reconstructing source for games whose code was lost, and remixing them. The uncomfortable half of the headline is the generalization: if agents can reliably reconstruct source from a game binary, they can do it to any paid software, and the practical obscurity that compiled distribution provided is gone. [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/)
