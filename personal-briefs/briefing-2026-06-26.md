# Daily Briefing — June 26, 2026

## From Your Inbox

**via Fix/Insider (Simon Andrews): US export controls on Anthropic "change everything."** Washington's order barring non-Americans from Anthropic's top models (Fable 5 and Mythos 5) — which forced Anthropic to disable both worldwide — has supercharged Europe's "technological sovereignty" push, with French politicians pressing to accelerate Mistral. Simon's read: stop fretting about access to frontier models you can't get and squeeze real value from what's available now. [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/6/19/us-export-ban-on-anthropics-ai-models-further-strains-alliances)

**via Fix/Insider: McKinsey says AI is dissolving the campaign-era marketing model.** The argument is that AI changes customer behavior so fundamentally that marketing has to be rebuilt as a continuous, AI-powered growth engine — insight, creation, personalization, and agentic commerce running in real time — with McKinsey pegging 4–7% revenue growth and 60–70% execution savings for those who get it right.

**via Fix/Insider: OpenAI went to Cannes and declared "we are clearly in the advertising business now."** CRO Denise Dresser said OpenAI wants its ad business measured not by impressions or time spent but by whether ads help people get things done — "moving from the attention economy to the intelligence economy."

**via Fix/Insider: Bain is vibecoding AI replicas of acquisition targets, and the "chief AI officer" gets called a transitional species.** Bain now tests software takeover candidates by building AI clones of them; separately, Fix flags the growing view that the CAIO role is a temporary scaffold that disappears once AI is woven into every function. Google's DeepMind also took a ~$75M stake in studio A24 to build AI filmmaking tools.

## Perspective

**via John Cutler (TBM 427, "The Bottleneck Strikes Again"):** Cutler is tired of the slideware cliché that, thanks to AI, "engineering is no longer the bottleneck" — noting the bottleneck has really just shifted to conversations about how the bottleneck has shifted. His point: engineering was never simply *the* bottleneck, and AI only reliably speeds things up in the minority of cases with lots of data, lots of reps, and strong situational awareness. [cutlefish.substack.com](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-427-the-bottleneck-strike-again)

**via Venkatesh Rao (Contraptions, "Strange Knowledgeability"):** Rao tries to name the eerie new kind of knowing LLMs embody — a "perspectival encyclopedicity." You can catch a model hallucinating, but you can't easily catch it *wrong-footed*: it groks every angle on a topic and offers resonance for any private, half-formed insight, helping you make your own thinking legible to yourself. [contraptions.venkateshrao.com](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/strange-knowledgeability)
