# Daily Briefing — July 20, 2026

## From Your Inbox

**via Ben Evans: "Feeling the elephant" — why your AI assistant can't really know you.** His lead essay this week: a truly useful assistant needs to see your whole life, but that life is siloed across Google, Meta, and Apple — and even open silos only expose endpoints, not the recommendation graphs behind them. The enterprise version is easier (demand APIs, connect Salesforce + telemetry + calls), creating a new network effect: value from more apps per assistant, not more users per app. [Newsletter #652](https://mailchi.mp/3aa7bdfc72b6/benedicts-newsletter-1213280)

**via Ben Evans: Kimi K3 was "the only topic in Silicon Valley this week."** Moonshot AI's open model isn't quite frontier but is only months behind; Evans cautions benchmarks are fuzzy and asks how many use-cases actually need the last 10% at today's prices. Related: Mira Murati's Thinking Machines shipped the first credible US open model since Llama floundered — partly distilled from Chinese models, an amusing inversion of US labs' distillation complaints.

**via Ben Evans: Stripe (with Advent) offers $53bn for PayPal.** The strategic logic is real — Stripe's B2B rails plus PayPal's two-sided network and checkout — but PayPal is a distressed asset in long decline, and the Collisons have never run anything but Stripe.

**via Ben Evans: semis diverge — TSMC and ASML up, IBM's worst day on record.** May global chip billings more than doubled YoY; TSMC raised guidance and capex. IBM warned as customers delay big-iron purchases while memory prices spike (capacity diverted to HBM for AI); the stock fell over 25%.

**via Ben Evans: Meta in preliminary talks to rent spare compute to Anthropic** (per NYT/CNBC) — a notable turn after weeks of signals Meta was open to becoming a compute landlord.

**via Ben Evans: the AI-doom drumbeat gets institutional.** Economists published an open letter on AI job impacts, and Demis Hassabis argued AGI is close and called for a US federal regulator to review frontier models pre-release. Evans' jab: by Silicon Valley's loosened definition of AGI, 1960s mainframes qualify too. Meanwhile New York passed the first US state moratorium on data-centre construction.

## Perspective

**via John Cutler: TBM 431 — The Denominator That Matters.** AI success in orgs is multiplicative: machine understanding × problem understanding × practice evolution — any term at zero kills the product, which is why "everyone must use AI" mandates fail. But the denominator is the social contract: a credible commitment that gains won't be captured against the people generating them. When it's broken, resistance isn't incompetence — it's a rational reading of the evidence. [Read it](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-431-the-denominator-that-matters)

## AI & Tech News

**EU orders Google to open Android to rival AI assistants and share search data.** Binding DMA decisions (July 16): third-party assistants get voice activation and cross-app capabilities across 11 Android feature groups, and Google must share anonymized ranking/query/click data on FRAND terms — data sharing by Jan 2027, Android interop by mid-2027. Arguably the year's most consequential AI regulatory action. [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4198420/google-must-open-android-to-rival-ai-agents-eu-orders.html)

**Oracle cuts up to 30,000 jobs to fund Stargate.** Roughly 18% of its workforce, freeing an estimated $8–10bn/year for the $500bn buildout anchored by a $300bn OpenAI cloud contract — the most honest accounting yet of where AI capex actually comes from, and the most concentrated single-customer bet in enterprise tech. [Capacity](https://capacityglobal.com/news/oracle-cuts-up-to-30000-jobs-to-fund-ai-data-centre-push/)

**SAP closes its Prior Labs acquisition with €1bn+ committed.** The 18-month-old German lab behind TabPFN (tabular foundation models, published in Nature) will be scaled into a frontier lab — a contrarian bet that structured business data, not chatbots, is enterprise AI's biggest untapped opportunity. [SAP News](https://news.sap.com/2026/07/sap-completes-prior-labs-acquisition/)

**Gemini 3.5 Pro reportedly slips a third time.** Google is said to be weighing a stopgap Gemini 3.6 Flash while the Pro model gets fixed; Alphabet fell ~4% on the reports. Caveat: no official model card or confirmation exists, so this is all reporting. Paired with the EU order, a brutal week for both halves of Google's AI strategy. [Build Fast with AI roundup](https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/ai-news-today-july-20-2026-16-biggest-stories)
