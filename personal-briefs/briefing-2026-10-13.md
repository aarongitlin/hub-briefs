## From Your Inbox

**via Superhuman AI:** Meta shipped Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, its first frontier-class models. Worth tempering the framing: Superhuman says Wonder Full outranks Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, which is true (56 vs 53), but it omits that Claude Opus 5.5 still leads at 58. [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/10/ukraines-drones-knock-out-ai-data-center-belonging-to-russias-google/)

**via Ben Evans:** The reported $70bn OpenAI annualized revenue figure was wrong; the real number is "close to $50bn." The gap came from OpenAI reporting net of reseller payments while Anthropic reports gross, and nobody labeling which was which. His column argues the metrics themselves are dotcom-grade funny numbers, and that even a clean quarterly P&L wouldn't answer the question that matters: whether models capture value up the stack or settle into low-margin commodity infrastructure. A related knock-on — a wave of Q3 IPOs got pulled, partly because the pending Anthropic IPO is crowding the market.

**via Ben Evans:** Agents are hitting the plumbing problem. Meta launched a "Personal Agent Protocol" standard with the usual logo slide, while Amazon and other retailers are blocking agents outright. Evans's sharpest point: Amazon did $75bn in ad revenue last year, and the agent doesn't see the ads.

**via Ben Evans:** Credible open-weight models are no longer only Chinese. US startup Reflection shipped one, Mistral came back from the dead with another, and the FT reports Nvidia is looking at buying Reflection — accelerating adoption while fragmenting its own customer base.

**via Lenny's Newsletter (with Dan Hockenmaier):** A framework for judging whether a startup you're considering joining has a real moat. Only four "compounding advantages" count — switching costs, network effects, scale economies, data effects. Speed, distribution, brand, team and funding are fuel, not moats. The practical test: ignore the stated strategy and look at what the team actually works on every day. [Read it](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-tell-if-a-startup-has-a-moat)

## Perspective

**via The Product Model #314 (Rory Madden):** The interface has changed again. His argument: as agent traffic displaces human traffic, the API and MCP become the UX, and MCP-only competitors will enter industries the way mobile-only competitors did. The freeing part is the mindset shift — what can you now offer that was too complex to get right in a UI? ## AI & Tech News

**Character.AI filed for Chapter 11.** Five years in, after raising $193M, hitting unicorn status and 20M monthly actives. The pressure came from lawsuits — including one over a 14-year-old's suicide — a Texas child-safety investigation, and inference costs that rise with the engagement the product was built to maximize. Disney has bid $20M for some assets plus a perpetual non-exclusive license; the platform runs until the sale closes in late November or early December, and shuts down if no viable bid appears. Google had paid $2.7B in 2024 to license the models and rehire the founders. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/13/just-5-years-in-interactive-chatbot-platform-character-ai-files-for-bankruptcy/)

**Amex proposed an agent-payments standard.** The "Intent Intelligence Framework" has the customer authenticate and approve a purchase intent — merchant, budget, product constraints — after which Amex issues an Intent ID and a Proof of Intent Token the agent redeems for a one-time credential instead of the card number. This is the direct answer to the chargeback-liability fear that has retailers blocking agents, per the Evans item above. No named adopters yet. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/13/amex-introduces-a-framework-to-prevent-ai-agents-from-making-unauthorized-purchases/)
