## From Your Inbox

**via Fix (Simon Andrews):** The June 22 issue is a contrarian curation aimed squarely at AI-labor hype — pieces arguing that "AI will replace human workers in droves" is "a clever marketing tactic," that "AI absolutism is breaking our brains," and warning about AI used to surveil and micromanage workers. Frames a "wisdom stack" as the thing that actually sets people apart. [Link](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com)

**via HeyDesigner Weekly #749:** Atlassian published its DESIGN.md — an experiment in "portable design context," i.e. a markdown file that hands design intent to AI agents the way CLAUDE.md hands them code context. Early notes on what worked and what didn't in practice. [Link](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/ai-at-work/atlassians-design-md-is-here-what-we-learned-testing-portable-design-context-in-practice)

## Perspective

**via The Product Model #298 ("What Teams Risk Losing To AI"):** A themed issue on the hidden costs of AI-in-the-loop work. The standout: "cognitive surrender" — the tendency to accept AI outputs too quickly and too confidently, weakening judgment — alongside arguments for stronger review habits, citation/provenance in AI-driven decisions, and protecting how organizations actually learn. [Link](https://www.theproductmodel.com)

**via Anil Dash — "How we'll fight the platform war against Big AI":** Dash argues Big AI companies *can* lose, and that the counter isn't to commoditize the complement but to undercut them economically through open-source tooling — the harnesses, editors, and chat interfaces people actually use moving faster than the incumbents. A rare optimistic-but-concrete take on open alternatives. [Link](https://anildash.com/2026/06/23/fight-ai-platform-war/)
