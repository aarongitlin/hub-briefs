## AI & Tech News

**Apple ships its first touchscreen Macs.** Yesterday's "Pro and Tell" event confirmed the rumor: a 14-inch and 16-inch MacBook Pro with a tandem-OLED "PROLED XDR" touch display. It handles taps, pinches, and multi-finger gestures, like three fingers to switch desktops or two to snap a window to half the screen. There is no Apple Pencil support, and the screen doesn't fold flat. Prices start at $2,899 (14-inch) and $3,399 (16-inch), and they ship Nov 6. The glowing Apple logo is back too. This is a reversal of more than a decade of Apple saying touch doesn't belong on a laptop. [TechRadar hands-on](https://www.techradar.com/computing/macbooks/apples-touchscreen-macbook-pro-is-finally-here-and-yes-touching-a-macbook-screen-feels-totally-and-wonderfully-natural) · [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/future/technology/2026/10/28/macbook-pro-touch-glowing-apple/)

**Microsoft booked $10B from OpenAI last quarter.** That's almost 11% of Microsoft's $92.3B in quarterly revenue, and $24.1B for fiscal 2026. Its stake in OpenAI slipped to 24%, and OpenAI has committed to another $250B of Azure. Microsoft also disclosed Azure revenue in dollars for the first time: $33.2B. Read next to this week's Anthropic S-1, the pattern is clear. The hyperscalers are making real money from labs that are still burning cash. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/28/microsoft-recorded-10-billion-in-revenue-from-openai/) · [Azure figure](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/28/microsoft-just-shared-azures-actual-revenue-in-dollars-33-2-billion/)

**Anthropic launches "Smart Reports" to show what AI spend is buying.** These are enterprise reports that combine usage analytics with employee surveys to show a team's output against its token cost. They flag where workflows broke down and suggest fixes. Pooled group budgets come with it. The timing fits the IPO: enterprise buyers are asking about ROI, and Anthropic is selling them the measuring stick. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/28/anthropic-just-launched-tools-to-show-companies-what-their-ai-spending-is-buying/)

**Google opens Gemini and Search to third-party apps via MCP.** The new Connected Apps Platform lets developers plug apps into the Gemini app and Google Search through an MCP server or Android AppFunctions. It's in public preview for US developers via a waitlist. More than 50 apps, including Adobe and Peloton, already work in Gemini and come to Search in the coming weeks. It's Google's answer to ChatGPT's apps platform, and it's built on MCP (Model Context Protocol, the open standard for plugging tools into AI models). [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/28/as-ai-agents-reshape-the-web-google-opens-gemini-and-search-to-more-apps/)

**GitHub Octoverse 2026: the agent effect shows up in the numbers.** Merged pull requests hit 220M a month, about triple the level of under a year ago. Commits reached 7.38B a month, most of that growth in the second half of the year, and new repos doubled to 30.3M a month. Simon Willison attributes the jump to agentic coding. [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/28/github-octoverse-2026/)

**Wabi raises $55M at a $1.25B valuation.** It now calls itself a "personal agent" that builds throwaway mini-apps (meal plans, workout plans, date ideas) on request. IVP led, with a16z and the Alexa Fund in, plus angels Tobi Lütke and Marc Benioff. It's a big bet on disposable, made-for-one software. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/29/wabi-the-ai-agent-that-builds-mini-apps-now-valued-at-1-25b-after-latest-round/)

## Long Reads

**Simon Willison, "The October inflection point for vibe game development" (Oct 28).** Willison argues that this month, games made mostly by prompting got *fun*, not just good-looking, much like coding agents crossed a line last November. He shows four browser games: Tree Appreciator, one-shotted in Claude chat; Emberwick, an RPG; Battle Drones, an RTS that improved a lot after he prompted "Make it a LOT more fun"; and Pumpkin Farmer. All were built with Claude Opus 5.5, and he says GPT-6 Astra doesn't yet match its sense of fun. His conclusion: anyone can now make a playable game, and the interesting work will come from people who actually playtest.
