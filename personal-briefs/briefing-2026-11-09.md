## Long Reads

**Ben Evans, "Abstraction."** His thesis: a computer shouldn't ask a question it can work out itself. AI's real shift is moving people up a level, from "run this 500-line SQL query" to "which customers will churn?" Most enterprise AI so far just does the old workflows faster. The interesting part is inventing the new questions, the way Spotify invented "what if $15 a month gets you all the music?" That's a human job, which is why AI "changes both more and less than people think." [Issue](https://mailchi.mp/0430a1fc8c4b/benedicts-newsletter-1213713)

**Alex Heath interviews Tobi Lütke (via Fix).** The Shopify CEO tells AI startups to prove their agents create value before picking a business model, whether that's direct charges, metered usage or a cut of each sale. He calls agents paid to steer your purchases "a breach of trust," but sees room for a discovery-marketplace kind of agent advertising. [Sources](https://sources.news/p/tobi-lutke-shopify-ai-agents)
