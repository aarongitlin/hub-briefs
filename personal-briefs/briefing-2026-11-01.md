## Perspective

**via John Cutler (TBM 448): the "software factory" is an identity defense.** When AI makes producing software cheap, engineers move up a level. They become architects of the factory and control orchestration, quality gates, evals, and deploy pipelines. That is real craft and a bid to keep professional authority, both at once. Cutler shows the pattern is old: Wedgwood, Jacquard, and Taylor did it in industrialization, and NoOps/DevOps fought the same fight in 2012. His sharper point: every function is now building its own factory (software, PM, GTM, content), and each one stops at its own professional border. "Poor design is sitting somewhere in the middle wondering WTF is going on." We're reimagining work one silo at a time. [The Beautiful Mess](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-448-the-software-factory-as-identity)

**via Venkatesh Rao (Contraptions): "Experiments with Monstrousness."** Rao reintroduces himself to the 1,000+ readers his viral "Cognitive Mercantilism" essay brought in. His motto, "Be Slightly Monstrous," means adopting AI without reservation and letting it change you. He no longer labels AI-assisted writing; hand-written is now the exception that needs a label (this post is one). He uses vanilla ChatGPT, not a fine-tune, and edits AI text as lightly as he'd edit a human collaborator, tics and all. His claim is that 90% of it is courage and only 10% is technique. The provocative turn: clinging to a 2024 snapshot of "the human" may be the deadening choice, and co-evolving with the tech may be where the liveness is. He sets this against what he calls "Humanism Derangement Syndrome." It's a deliberately one-sided case. [Contraptions](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/experiments-with-monstrousness)

## Long Reads

**Parameter Labs, "How We Took Over ChatGPT Accounts" (Oct 31).** A clear walk-through of a chained exploit against ChatGPT's sandboxed HTML previews. The chain abused MathLive's virtual keyboard through postMessage and innerHTML, then used a YouTube oEmbed endpoint to run JavaScript past OpenAI's CSP. A crafted share link could pull a signed-in visitor's private data. OpenAI fixed it and paid a $5,000 bounty, which Simon Willison calls "a bit cheap." Required reading for anyone who renders model-generated HTML.
