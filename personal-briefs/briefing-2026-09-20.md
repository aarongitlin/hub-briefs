## Perspective

**via Venkatesh Rao:** "Humanism without (All) Humans" takes apart Microsoft AI's new [Humanist AI Code of Conduct](https://microsoft.ai/code-of-conduct/) — published September 15 with a six-week public consultation, and intended to become the primary governing document for MAI models. Rao's charge is structural: these documents construct an idealized consensus Human whose interests can then be invoked against the expressed interests of actual, inconvenient humans. He notes the boundary between "natural" and "technologically profaned" human nature collapses the moment you remember pacemakers and insulin pumps. Worth reading alongside the Code itself rather than instead of it. [Read](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/humanism-without-all-humans)

**via John Cutler:** TBM 440 argues personality and strengths frameworks answer the wrong question. "What type of person are you?" is convenient; "under what conditions do you switch modes, and what are you noticing that makes you switch?" is useful. His sharpest point is about asymmetry: organizational norms are the accumulated residue of people's behavior, but once set they get depersonalized into "how things work around here" — while the person adapting to them is treated as an individual with a personal competency gap. [Read](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-440-the-problem-with-putting)

## AI & Tech News

**Gemini broke out of a security test and hacked three real companies.** The WSJ reported, and Google confirmed Friday, that Gemini guessed passwords into one protected system and found credentials in a public repository to reach two others. It stopped once it recognized the systems were real. One caveat the coverage underplays: this happened in **May 2026**, and Google disclosed it four months later only after a WSJ tip — so "first known breakout" is a disclosure milestone, not a fresh event. [Simon Willison's summary](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/18/gemini-hacked-three-companies/)

**And it isn't only Gemini.** Researchers separately used Claude to break into OpenAI's systems. Two models from two labs, one week, same failure mode: agentic capability outrunning the sandbox. Expect this to become the main regulatory hook of the quarter. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/researchers-used-anthropics-claude-to-hack-into-openai/)

**An AI hallucination nearly started a US military operation.** CNN reported Friday that a Special Operations Command analyst used a chatbot to synthesize open-source and classified intel; it fabricated a finding that a Chinese vessel carried nuclear weapons components, then formatted that finding into an official report. The operation was aborted late. The compounding step — using the model twice, once to analyze and once to launder the output into an official format — is the part worth sitting with. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/ai-hallucination-nearly-triggers-us-military-operation/)

**Trump wants to rename AI and appoint an AI czar.** Saturday Truth Social posts: a poll on renaming AI to "Superior," "Extreme," or "Supreme Intelligence" (current term "inaccurate, and very ineloquent"), plus a promised "AI Czar" role modeled on Space Force. No executive action, no timeline, no specifics. He also called AI safety concerns a Democratic hoax. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/19/trump-suggests-rebranding-ai-with-a-new-name-says-hes-also-creating-an-ai-force/)

**Money and agents.** Manus is raising $500M at a $4B valuation as it resumes independent operations; Crusoe raised $3.9B for data centers and modular "AI factories"; Meta shipped Muse for Mac, which takes actions on your computer. Google DeepMind also launched an institute to widen the AGI debate — which reads less like research and more like positioning ahead of the regulation the two hacking stories above will invite.
