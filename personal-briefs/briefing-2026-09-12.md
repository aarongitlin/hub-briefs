## AI & Tech News

**Twenty-five Fields Medallists signed a declaration against how AI labs are doing mathematics.** Terence Tao published it Friday under the title "A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics," co-signed by Artur Avila, Manjul Bhargava and 22 others. The argument is not that AI can't do math — it's that labs optimize for famous problems as benchmarks while mathematics runs on conceptual understanding and transmission. Tao's line: "mass production at faster and faster pace of 'true/false' statements could destroy fertile ground instead of breathing life into new ideas." Rushed announcements skip the writeup, the isolation of new methods, and the citation of prior work, which breaks both attribution and the training of the next generation. This lands on top of the Navier–Stokes fight, which escalated the same week: OpenAI pulled its sponsorship of a CalTech math event Thursday after researchers there objected, and Buckmaster now says OpenAI pressured him not to credit his Anthropic collaborator. [Tao's post](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/09/11/a-severe-misalignment-of-ai-in-mathematics/) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/11/openais-feud-with-mathematicians-is-only-escalating/)

**Garry Tan wants an American distillation regime — a direct shot at Amodei.** Days after Anthropic's report on Chinese distillation campaigns and Dario Amodei's call for a regulatory crackdown, YC's CEO says he'd "do nothing," and goes further: US open-weight labs should be allowed to distill frontier models too, because those models were trained on public human knowledge and "access to capable AI should be a form of public good." He draws the line at stolen credentials, not at API outputs. The tell is his stated fear — "the nightmare scenario is that there's just one company." Two coherent positions, incompatible, both now on the record ahead of the next export-control round. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/11/y-combinators-garry-tan-wants-u-s-open-weight-ai-labs-to-distill-frontier-models-too/)

**If you route through OpenRouter, your model isn't one model.** Mohamed Moustafa's analysis, surfaced by Simon Willison: the same model ID behaves differently depending on which backend provider serves it — some lack vision, reasoning effort is handled inconsistently, configurations vary. The `provider.only` option and the `/endpoints` method let you pin it down. A small operational note with real teeth if you've been treating OpenRouter's fallback logic as transparent. [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/11/so-you-want-to-use-openrouter/)

## Long Reads

**Simon Willison, "Feeling sad about AI"** (Sept 11). A response to a Hacker News thread about the dread of watching an agent finish in hours what would have taken a week. Willison's counter is neither dismissal nor comfort: the shock is real and it passes, and what's actually gone is translating an exact specification into decent code, which was never the scarce skill. His claim that experienced practitioners hold the advantage — depth of knowledge is what lets you master a new tool fast — is the part worth arguing with rather than nodding at. [Read it](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/11/feeling-sad-about-ai/)

**Scott Alexander, "Royce On San Francisco"** (Sept 10). Long excerpts from Josiah Royce's 1886 history of Gold Rush California, on a boomtown of speculative housing, unstable families, ambient crime, and a civic culture whose real failure Royce locates not in vice but in "tolerance of the open vices." The pleasure here is the double vision — every complaint reads like a 2026 city thread, which is either reassuring or the opposite. [Read it](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/royce-on-san-francisco)
