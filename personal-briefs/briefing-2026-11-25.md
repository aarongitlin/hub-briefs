## Perspective

**via Venkatesh Rao: "The Future Needs Furniture."** Rao answers Yancey Strickler's lament about AI "breaking the world." His image: we moved from a tent into a mansion that has no furniture yet. "Furniture" means the stable categories, protocols and habits that let us act without rethinking reality every morning. He names an "ontological crisis of the third kind," where you still care about distinctions you can no longer act on. His fix is to map which domains are *playable*, not which agents are capable. Machines win on playable islands. Meaning comes from grinding through the unplayable parts, with others. Long and dense, but the furniture idea is worth having. [Contraptions](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/the-future-needs-furniture)

**via The Product Model: 2026 was agents, 2027 is "engines."** An agent finishes a task and stops. An engine gets an outcome and loops on its own (do, check, fix) until it meets it. Uber's "software factory," Snap and Sephora are all building versions. The catch: an engine is only as good as its definition of "done." Most teams keep that in people's heads, in design reviews and PM gut feel. Sephora's Sneha Narahalli: "Let AI earn autonomy." Start with one loop you already run by hand. The same issue links John Cutler's "Contexting vs. Context," which argues shared context can't just be written down for people or AI. It grows through working together. [The Product Model #320](https://blog.uxdx.com/r/eb875896?m=7d89cb24-ba29-4ec9-aaa7-55c6e2d9766f) · [Cutler](https://blog.uxdx.com/r/63b3f694?m=7d89cb24-ba29-4ec9-aaa7-55c6e2d9766f)
