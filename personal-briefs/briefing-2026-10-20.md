## Perspective

**via The Beautiful Mess (John Cutler), TBM 446 "Halos, Vibes, Causality, and Identity":** Cutler separates four ways people use causal claims: a working hypothesis, identity reinforcement, an explanation of what happened, and a view of what changes the odds. Management books and tech's "vibe-osphere" mix all four, so the doctrine wins every time: success proves it, failure means you didn't commit, and skepticism means you lack the mindset. The practical takeaway is that a lot of talk about merit, agency, and grit is closer to secular theology than evidence. You can stop arguing a causal point with someone who is really defending a worldview, and you can admit your own worldview is part of the causal story too. [Read](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-446-halos-vibes-causality-and)

**via The Product Model #315, "From Jobs to Outcomes":** As Muse, Cowork, and ChatGPT Work start doing tasks for customers instead of helping with them, Jobs to Be Done shifts from single tasks to whole outcomes. Rory Madden echoes Des Traynor: functionality stops being a moat, and software companies will move into each other's territory. Customers will still pay instead of vibe-coding everything, but their expectations for quality and breadth will rise fast. The issue also links Marty Cagan's "Predictability vs Outcomes" and Cassidy Williams on why AI agents make accessibility worth more. [Product Model](https://blog.uxdx.com/r/40da7cd7?m=7d89cb24-ba29-4ec9-aaa7-55c6e2d9766f)

They ran up charges on OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google accounts, sometimes over $1,000. OpenCode calls it a "distillation attack," but hasn't said how attackers got in, and observers dispute that label. The open-source CLI and app weren't affected. If you got an email from OpenCode, rotate your keys directly with each provider. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/19/hackers-hit-ai-coding-tool-opencode-running-up-charges-on-users-ai-accounts/)
