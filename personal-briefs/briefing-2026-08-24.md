# Daily Briefing — Monday, August 24, 2026

## From Your Inbox

**via Ben Evans: Stripe buys OpenRouter for ~$8bn.** Evans reads this as the first real piece of strategic M&A up the stack rather than another model-lab land grab — if models commoditize, routing and brokerage becomes the toll booth, and taking a few basis points on every AI transaction is exactly Stripe's muscle memory. ([newsletter](https://www.ben-evans.com/newsletter))

**via Ben Evans + Fix: the labs start reporting real quarters, and Anthropic passes OpenAI.** Per the WSJ, OpenAI did $5.7bn in Q1 and $6.7bn in Q2 (18% growth, deepening losses); Anthropic went $4.8bn → $11.6bn and swung to a small operating profit. Bloomberg separately puts Anthropic's annualized run rate above $65bn by end of July. Both writers attribute the gap to agentic coding PMF. ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/openais-second-quarter-sales-show-tepid-growth-compared-with-anthropic-5cb42998))

**via Ben Evans: "The Bank of Jensen" — three Nvidia deals in one week.** A $105bn guarantee toward the OpenAI/SoftBank Ohio data centre, $1.5bn into SB Energy plus a reported $3bn into Lacium, and a $7bn Poolside arrangement ($1bn stake + $6bn license-and-acquihire). Evans' read: Nvidia is recycling its customers' cash to make its customers cheaper to finance, more dependent on CUDA, and played off against each other.

**via Ben Evans: Unitree IPO pops ~630%, to a $50bn valuation.** He's on the perplexed side — the FT notes most shipments (25–50k units this year, per Morgan Stanley) go to Chinese government "research centres." His framing is worth stealing: intelligence is the hard part, not walking. How many use cases need a manipulator but genuinely need legs?

**via Ben Evans: "Ways to think about token pricing."** His own essay on what happens when the AI supply crunch ends — why the labs might *not* end up as low-margin commodity infrastructure, and where price, capacity and capex re-equilibrate. Probably the most useful strategic read in the inbox this week.

**via Fix: ChatGPT Ads expands to 31 European countries next week.** Germany, France, Spain, Italy, the Nordics and more. Free and Go tiers only; Plus/Pro/Enterprise stay ad-free. Access is via OpenAI's ads team and agency partners, with self-serve Ads Manager "later this summer" — Simon Andrews' note is that they need to hurry on self-serve. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-ads-expands-across-europe/))

**via Fix + Ben Evans: Uber and Zipline target one million drone deliveries a day by 2029.** Dallas and Houston by end of year, with Uber taking an equity stake. Khosrowshahi: five-to-ten minute fulfillment vs ~30 today, and "truly quick commerce is proving to be an even bigger market than the original food market was." Amazon's Prime Air is expanding to ~500 US towns in parallel. ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/tech/uber-zipline-partner-with-goal-of-hitting-1-million-drone-deliveries-a-day-e022b67a))

**via Fix: Reed Hastings on the coming creative destruction.** His arithmetic: if AI keeps roughly doubling annually, in five years it's ~32x more effective, which is like doubling your workforce every year. The strategic fork he poses — halve headcount and stay in your lane, or spend the surplus attacking adjacent competitors — is the more interesting half. ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/08/13/2026/reed-hastings-ceo-signal))

**via Fix + Ben Evans: Meta's trillion-dollar social media addiction trial opens.** States are seeking more than $1tn. Evans is notably unwilling to run the tobacco analogy: there's no scientific consensus on tangible, specific harms of the kind that existed for smoking and cancer. ([Sky News](https://news.sky.com/story/meta-goes-on-federal-trial-for-social-media-addiction-with-states-seeking-more-than-1trn-13574223))

**via Design Better: designing for a future that doesn't exist yet.** A roundup built around Nick Foster (ex-Google X, now consulting with Anthropic) on the disposition futures work demands — comfort with ambiguity, cross-disciplinary friction over designer groupthink, and Ford's Jae Park on "the valley" between one era of work and the next. Best line, from Stanford d.school's Scott Doorley: "when you're overwhelmed, it's really hard to be curious. And when you're curious, it's really hard to be overwhelmed." ([post](https://designbetterpodcast.com/p/the-roundup-designing-for-a-future))

## AI & Tech News

**Google's A2A protocol formally joins the Agentic AI Foundation, landing next to MCP.** Agent-to-agent coordination and agent-to-tool access now sit under the same Linux Foundation–directed governance, with AAIF reportedly past 250 members including AWS, Anthropic, Cloudflare, Google, Microsoft and OpenAI. The proprietary-protocol-silo era for agents looks like it's over before it properly started. ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/janakirammsv/2026/08/19/agent2agent-joins-the-agentic-ai-foundation-alongside-mcp/), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/17/a2a-agentic-ai-foundation-open-ai-standards))

**The data centre backlash is now electoral, not just rhetorical.** Beyond the polling Ben Evans cited: Abbott's early-August Texas moratorium has reportedly halted up to 1,800 projects, New York and Pennsylvania have added stoppages or restrictions, and residents in more than a dozen communities are pushing recall elections against officials who approved builds. Opposition is genuinely bipartisan — GOP on tax incentives and grid strain, Democrats on water and emissions. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/20/ai-data-center-election-backlash.html))
