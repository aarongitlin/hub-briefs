# Daily Briefing — September 21, 2026

Monday. Ben Evans's Sunday issue carries most of the weight today. The week's through-line is money: what frontier labs actually earn, what enterprises actually get back, and why the loudest safety pitch is coming from the company in the lead.

## From Your Inbox

**via Ben Evans: Anthropic's "gross gross margin."** The FT reports Anthropic told investors it runs 80% gross margins *before* paying distribution partners like AWS, plus positive adjusted operating income *before* stock-based comp. Its S-1, expected last week, has reportedly slipped toward year-end. Evans compares it to the telcos of ~2002 and their "EBITDA before customer acquisition costs." His real point: nobody knows how fast token prices fall or how much pricing power a lab keeps, so today's margin says little. OpenAI's FT-reported plan is the same kind of illustration: $278bn of negative free cash flow from 2026 to 2030, and $350bn of revenue in 2030. **via Ben Evans: Suleyman says Anthropic's consciousness research is circular.** Microsoft AI's Mustafa Suleyman wrote that training a model to talk as if it has feelings, then "observing" that it seems to have feelings, measures nothing but your own setup. Evans agrees and applies the same critique to OpenAI's new misalignment framework: these are bugs, and nobody said Word "decided" to eat your document. This is the other half of the Microsoft vs. Anthropic argument Venkatesh Rao wrote about Saturday. [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/16/microsoft-ai-chief-anthropic-consciousness)

**via Ben Evans + Superhuman: the personal-agent race now has numbers.** Meta's Muse is the #1 free app in the US App Store, and Zuckerberg announced [Muse connectors](https://muse.ai/platform) so companies can plug their services into it. Evans reports invite-only Instinct is raising $1bn at a $10bn valuation with about 100k active users. That backs up the Instinct figure we flagged as unconfirmed on Sept 11. His comparison: Meta paid $1bn for Instagram at 30m users, and people called that crazy. Evans also doubts that Muse is a mainstream activity even with all of Meta's growth machine behind it.

**via Ben Evans: the column, "Leverage and averages."** Two questions for any industry. First: does AI hit a structural point of leverage, the way the internet hit newspapers' printing-and-trucking moat? Or is it just a tool, the way email was for mining companies? An "AI-enabled law firm" might be no more structural than a "PC-enabled" one. Second: models give you the *average* answer. That's ideal for taxes, and when scaled it becomes the "agentic swarm" that tries every exploit at once. It's useless when you need an insight that doesn't match the training data. "It knows what people have liked, but not what people like." The best thing in today's inbox.

**via Ben Evans: Apple's quiet AI moves beat the folding phone.** Apple Watch can now listen all day on-device and keep text summaries of conversations (no audio, no transcript, 7-day default retention). Evans expects courts to decide whether an AI summary of speech is admissible evidence. Also new: the iPhone 18 Pro can sign photos at the image sensor to prove they're real, and Apple is building a scam-detection API that other apps can call. [Apple Support: Audio Intelligence](https://support.apple.com/en-us/148354)

**via Superhuman AI: Claude Projects become a coordinator.** You can give a project several tasks at once. Claude plans the work, splits it into parallel threads, reviews the results, and assembles them, with shared memory that builds over time. It's in beta for some Pro/Max users on Claude Code cloud sessions, with wider rollout to follow. This is Sept 17 news, not fresh. Evans's jab from the same week is funnier: Anthropic opened its own biology wet lab while warning that AI might build killer viruses. **via Superhuman AI: enterprise AI ROI is mostly vibes.** Headline stat: 90% of organizations use AI, but 6% say they capture enterprise value. That comes from a vendor blog, so treat it loosely. Blame goes partly to "tokenmaxxing": companies that encouraged open-ended experimentation, Uber among them, blew through their budgets. The fixes are boring: per-employee usage dashboards with token caps, and routing most tasks to cheaper models. ## Long Reads

**Stratechery, "Frontier Overhangs"** (Sep 21). Thompson reads "Pace the Frontier" as a business strategy. He lists five overhangs. *Capability*: models are already good enough. *Product*: Muse shows a non-frontier model can win on integration. *Pricing*: today's prices reflect supply limits, and slowing down helps keep them. *Capital*: runways are finite. *Safety*: pacing may make cyber risk worse by giving attackers a longer lead over defenders. His conclusion is that Anthropic is happy to slow down the field while it leads. Pairs with Evans's margin item above. [Read](https://stratechery.com/2026/frontier-overhangs/)

**Bloomberg, "Inside US Military 'Kill Chain' That Destroyed an Iranian School"** (flagged by Ben Evans). Pentagon investigators found the February strike on a school in Minab killed 123 children. Causes: stale satellite images, time pressure, and over-reliance on Palantir's Maven analysis that nobody checked properly. Two of those are old problems. The third is new. Read it next to last week's near-boarding of a Chinese ship based on a hallucinated intel report. (Evans's issue says "last year." Other outlets reporting the Pentagon findings put it in February.)
