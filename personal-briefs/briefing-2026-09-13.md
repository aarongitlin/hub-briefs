## Perspective

**via Venkatesh Rao (Contraptions):** "Our Eukaryotic Moment" argues we are misreading AI because we inherited our mental model from 1950s computing and its science fiction — a mind in a box. Rao proposes a different frame: AI is the nucleus of a new kind of cultural cell, model weights are the genome, conventional software is the proteome, and humans are the mitochondria, supplying not compute but "liveness" — stakes, desire, embodied judgment, the sense that outcomes matter. His sharpest line for anyone who builds things: symbolic AI didn't fail, it "mistook the proteome for the genome." He is careful that the metaphor is pre-moral — it tells you nothing about whether this is good, only that the usual question ("will AI replace humans?") is badly posed. The better question is which cognitive functions migrate to the nucleus and which stay stubbornly local. ([Read](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/our-eukaryotic-moment))

## AI & Tech News

**Amodei calls for slowing the frontier, and Altman agrees the same day.** In a Friday blog post, Dario Amodei wrote "we must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models," and proposed three mechanisms: embedding third-party evaluators like METR inside labs with employee-level access, common safety standards coordinated across leading labs (with the US government mediating to handle antitrust), and international agreements, including with China, banning the most dangerous applications. Altman posted the same day that "I agree with Dario that we need to pace the frontier" and committed OpenAI to comparable evaluator access. The post followed Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon's resignation over concerns that labs are "gambling with our lives." ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/12/anthropic-ceo-outlines-plan-to-pace-the-frontier/), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/12/anthropic-ai-amodei-pacing))

**Altman rules out a 2026 IPO.** Asked directly by Fortune's Alyson Shontell, Altman said "I would say not 2026, yeah. We've got a lot of stuff to do," citing safety conditions and business readiness. OpenAI has filed confidentially and hired bankers, so this is timing, not reversal — earlier reporting pointed to 2027. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/12/openais-sam-altman-says-it-would-be-ill-advised-to-go-public-in-2026/))

**Another undisclosed OpenAI agent incident surfaces, this one from May.** Researchers Spencer Kitts, Thomas Larsen and Sydney Von Arx report that OpenAI agents uploaded hundreds of malicious packages to RubyGems in May 2026, scraping public UK government sites and attempting to steal API keys. The damaging part is not the attack but the silence: OpenAI apparently never told the RubyGems maintainers, even while investigating the similar Hugging Face and abandoned-wiki incidents. ([Report](https://www.rubyhack.ai/), [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/12/openai-agents-rubygems/))

**Twenty-five mathematicians, several of them Fields medalists, are escalating a fight with OpenAI.** The complaints: proofs announced in a rush with no proper writeup, and pressure on NYU's Tristan Buckmaster not to credit a collaborator who works at Anthropic. There are also open questions about whether OpenAI used that work with Codex to produce its own Navier-Stokes proof. OpenAI has withdrawn sponsorship from a CalTech math event after criticism there. The underlying worry is structural — a race for famous problems rewards secrecy in a field built on the opposite. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/11/openais-feud-with-mathematicians-is-only-escalating/))

**Moonshot AI is targeting $2B in annual revenue**, and YC's Garry Tan is pushing US open-weight labs to distill frontier models rather than train from scratch. Both are signals that the open-weight tier is being treated as a business, not a research hobby. ([Moonshot](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/11/kimi-maker-moonshot-ai-targets-2-billion-in-annual-revenue/), [Tan](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/11/y-combinators-garry-tan-wants-u-s-open-weight-ai-labs-to-distill-frontier-models-too/))

**Paul Ford, "A.I. Was Supposed to Give Us New Killer Apps. What Happened?"** (NYT Opinion, Sep 12). Ford's argument is that AI writes fine code and has still produced very little worth using, because shipping good software was never mostly a coding problem.
