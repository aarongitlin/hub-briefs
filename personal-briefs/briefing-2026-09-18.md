# Daily Briefing — September 18, 2026

## From Your Inbox

**via Design Better (Aarron Walter):** [7 principles for better AI collaboration](https://designbetterpodcast.com/p/the-brief-7-principles-for-better). Walter interviewed nine agents he works with and asked what he was doing wrong. The recurring answers: state goals and values rather than tasks, make the agent interview you before it builds, and when you repeat yourself the problem is a missing reference, not a missing emphasis. He also ships a downloadable kickoff prompt, a project brief template, and an installable skill.

OpenAI paired this with a new framework for catching and reporting the behavior. Ars Technica has [the detail](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/covert-uploads-and-megalomania-openai-details-new-misaligned-agent-incidents/).

## Perspective

**via Venkatesh Rao (Contraptions):** [EA Safety](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/ea-safety) is the week's most interesting essay and the hardest to summarize. Rao's argument: the EA formation is good at expressing uncertainty numerically and bad at doubting the model that generates the numbers, which makes it fragile exactly where it looks strongest. Its longtermist wing supplies stakes so large that any ordinary consideration can be rounded to zero. He is careful to separate radical philosophy from criminal deviancy rather than lumping Yudkowsky in with Bankman-Fried, and lands on pluralism: no faction, EA or e/acc or anyone else, gets to settle the moral meaning of AI for everybody. "AI safety is a real problem. EA safety now makes it two problems."

## AI & Tech News

**Researchers used Claude to break into OpenAI.** A three-person team at Hacktron AI chained two bugs, starting with a memory flaw in how Discourse forum software handled iPhone HEIF images, to reach employee ChatGPT accounts and OpenAI's GitHub org. Claude Opus 4.8 failed at the problem; Opus 5 solved it within hours of release. Discourse patched on July 27, OpenAI paid $6,500 through its bug bounty. Worth noting the hole was in third-party forum software, not OpenAI's core systems, which the headlines blur. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/researchers-used-anthropics-claude-to-hack-into-openai/)

**Crusoe raised $3.9B at a $30.9B valuation.** The Series F funds both hyperscale data centers and small modular "AI factories." Infrastructure money is still arriving faster than anything it's meant to serve. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/crusoe-raises-3-9b-to-build-massive-data-centers-and-small-modular-ai-factories/)

**Google DeepMind launched the DeepMind Institute** to widen the AGI debate beyond the labs, built around Shane Legg and publishing essays from outside voices. Read it alongside Rao above: this is a lab trying to visibly not own the moral framing, which is either the containment he's calling for or a very well-designed version of the problem. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/google-deepmind-launches-institute-to-widen-the-agi-debate/)

## Long Reads

**Thomas Ptacek, [How To Write With An LLM](https://sockpuppet.org/blog/2026/09/17/how-to-write-with-an-llm/).** The counterintuitive rule: use the model constantly, but never keep a phrase it suggested. He treats the LLM as an editor and an adversary rather than a drafter. Short, opinionated, and the most practical thing written on the subject in a while.

**Zvi Mowshowitz, [We Must Pace The Frontier](https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2026/09/14/we-must-pace-the-frontier/).** A close reading of the Amodei essay from someone who has spent years on this and does not hand out agreement cheaply. Useful for separating what Amodei actually proposed from what the coverage says he proposed.
