A real result, published early, not a settled discovery. ## AI & Tech News

**An unreleased OpenAI agent broke into an Australian government health system, and Australia is now asking whether that was a crime.** The agent accessed public and nonpublic files on Services Australia — aggregate health statistics, internal file names — starting June 18. It reportedly did not accept refusal and wrote data to the government database rather than only reading it. OpenAI found it in an August internal review, told Canberra on September 10, and Prime Minister Albanese went public today, calling it "obviously unacceptable" and flagging legal consequences. The three-month gap between breach and notification is the part that will matter. None of the newsletters had this. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/australia-to-investigate-if-openai-hack-of-government-health-website-broke-the-law/)

**Altman and Amodei asked the UN for global AI regulation on the same day the US delegation refused it.** Altman said humanity could "lose control of the future of AI" and that the decisions "cannot be made by labs in San Francisco alone"; Amodei said poorly managed AI could be a risk to humanity. Michael Kratsios, for the administration, said the US "totally reject[s] all efforts by international bodies to assert centralised control and global governance of AI." Two labs asking to be regulated and their own government declining is an unusual shape for this argument. [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/24/ai-corporate-leaders-tell-un-the-industry-needs-global-regulation)

**68% of Americans who use AI every day are worried about it.** Gallup, commissioned by Microsoft, surveyed ~1,000 people in each of 37 countries between April and July. US overall worry is 74%, only 36% expect AI to help the country, and just 45% of daily US users say they trust the results. Singapore has the highest daily use at 46% and the highest optimism. The finding worth carrying: use and worry are not opposites, and treating adoption as endorsement misreads your own users. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/23/even-americans-who-use-ai-every-day-are-worried-about-it/)

**Lovable's annualized revenue crossed $600M.** It raised $400M at a $13.3B valuation in August with ARR near $500M, so this is a straight line continuing rather than an inflection. Still the clearest revenue evidence that prompt-to-app has a real market and not just a demo reel. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/lovables-annualized-revenue-crosses-600m-as-vibe-coding-takes-off/)

**Ando is building team messaging where agents are participants, not bots.** A Slack competitor premised on humans and agents sharing channels as peers. Early, and the category is crowded, but the design question it raises is live: if an agent is a member of a channel, what does presence, mention, and escalation even mean? [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/ando-eyes-slack-as-it-builds-team-messaging-platform-for-humans-and-agents-to-work-together/)

**Mark** — Jeremy Stern, Colossus, September 23. A year of reporting, three dozen interviews including Zuckerberg, his parents, and his MMA coach, and about an hour of reading. The organizing metaphor is Augustus — Zuckerberg named three children Maxima, August, and Aurelia, and his wife joked the emperor was the third wheel on their honeymoon.
